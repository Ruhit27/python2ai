A metabolic state in which the body, short of [carbohydrate](./Carbohydrate.md), breaks down [fat](./Fat.md) into molecules called *ketones* and uses them as a major fuel, including for the brain. It happens during fasting and on very low-carb diets — typically under about 50 g of carbs a day.

The **ketogenic diet** (keto) aims to keep the body in ketosis by eating very little carbohydrate, moderate [protein](./Protein.md), and lots of fat. It has medical uses — notably for some forms of epilepsy — and some people find it helps control appetite.

For weight loss, research finds keto works about as well as other diets when [calories](./Calorie.md) are matched. The fast early drop is largely [water weight](./Water%20weight.md) from glycogen stores. It's hard to follow long-term, can limit [fiber](./Fiber.md) and some [micronutrients](./Micronutrient.md), and may reduce high-intensity exercise performance. Nutritional ketosis is different from *ketoacidosis*, a dangerous condition mainly in uncontrolled type 1 diabetes.

_Avoid:_ "ketosis" and "ketoacidosis" as the same — ketosis from diet is normal; ketoacidosis is a medical emergency.

_Usage:_

"Does keto burn more fat?"

"You burn more fat as fuel, but you also eat more fat. Fat loss still depends on your calorie deficit."
