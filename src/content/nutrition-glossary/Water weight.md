description: Short-term changes in body weight caused by shifts in stored water, not fat. Salt, carbs, and hormones all move it.
---

Changes in body weight caused by the body holding more or less water, rather than gaining or losing fat or muscle. It's why weight can jump or drop by a kilogram or two (2–4 lb) from one day to the next.

Common causes: a salty meal ([sodium](./Sodium.md) holds water); eating more carbohydrates (each gram of stored glycogen holds about 3 g of water); starting [creatine](./Creatine.md) (which draws water into muscles); hormonal cycles; hard training and soreness; and [hydration](./Hydration.md) levels.

It's also why the first week of a low-carb diet or a [cut](./Cutting.md) often shows a big drop that then slows down — much of it is water leaving with glycogen. The fix for reading the scale is to weigh in regularly and look at the weekly average, not single days.

_Avoid:_ "I gained 2 kg (4.5 lb) of fat this weekend" — you can't gain that much fat that fast; it's mostly water and food.

_Usage:_

"I'm up 1.5 kg (3 lb) after one big dinner. Diet ruined?"

"That's water weight from the salt and carbs. Give it three days."
