description: A mineral and electrolyte, mostly eaten as salt. Needed for nerves and fluid balance; most people eat more than recommended.
---

A [mineral](./Mineral.md) and key [electrolyte](./Electrolyte.md) that helps control fluid balance, nerve signals, and muscle contractions. Most of it comes from salt (sodium chloride) — about 40% of salt by weight is sodium.

Most people eat much more than health guidelines recommend. The World Health Organization suggests under 2,000 mg of sodium a day (about 5 g of salt, or one teaspoon), and many people eat nearly double that, mostly from processed and restaurant food rather than the salt shaker. High intakes are linked with raised blood pressure in many people.

Sodium also holds water in the body: a salty meal can raise the scale by a kilogram (2 lb) or more overnight in [water weight](./Water%20weight.md), which disappears within days. People who sweat heavily in long or hot training lose significant sodium and may need to replace it.

_Avoid:_ "salt" and "sodium" as the same number — 1 g of salt contains about 0.4 g of sodium. Labels may show either.

_Usage:_

"I gained a kilo (2 lb) overnight after pizza."

"That's the sodium holding water, not fat. It'll be gone in a couple of days."
