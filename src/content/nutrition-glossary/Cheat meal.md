description: Gym slang for a planned meal that breaks your usual diet rules. Can help some people stick to a plan; can backfire for others.
---

Slang for a meal that deliberately breaks the rules of your usual [diet](./Diet.md) — often pizza, burgers, or dessert — usually planned once a week during a [cut](./Cutting.md). A whole day of it is a *cheat day*. Some coaches prefer the term *free meal* or *refeed* (a planned day of higher carbohydrates, which is more controlled).

For some people, a planned break makes a strict plan easier to follow. But the effect on progress depends on size: one large meal can erase several days of a [calorie deficit](./Calorie%20deficit.md), and "cheat days" can reach thousands of extra calories. The claim that a cheat meal "boosts metabolism" is not well supported — any effect is small and short-lived.

The scale often jumps the next day, mostly from [water weight](./Water%20weight.md) (salt and carbs), not fat. Framing foods as "cheating" can also make some people feel guilty or swing between strict eating and overeating; flexible approaches that fit treats into a normal day tend to be more sustainable.

_Avoid:_ "cheat meals reset your metabolism" — there's little evidence; the real effect is on your weekly calorie total.

_Usage:_

"I was in a deficit all week, then had a cheat day. Why didn't I lose weight?"

"That day may have cancelled the whole week's deficit. Try one meal, not a whole day."
