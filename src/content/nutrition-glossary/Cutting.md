A planned period of eating in a [calorie deficit](./Calorie%20deficit.md) to lose body fat, usually while continuing to lift weights so that muscle is kept. Often done after a [bulk](./Bulking.md), or before an event.

From the nutrition side, the main question is how fast to go. Many coaches suggest losing about 0.5–1% of bodyweight a week: slower cuts keep more [lean mass](./Lean%20mass.md) and are easier to stick to; faster ones make hunger and muscle loss worse.

Useful strategies: high [protein](./Protein.md) intake (which helps protect muscle and boosts [satiety](./Satiety.md)), lots of vegetables and [fiber](./Fiber.md), low-[calorie-density](./Calorie%20density.md) foods, and planned breaks back to [maintenance](./Maintenance%20calories.md) on long cuts. The scale will bounce with [water weight](./Water%20weight.md), so weekly averages matter. The training side is covered in the Gym Glossary.

_Avoid:_ "cutting" as crash dieting — a good cut is slow and keeps muscle.

_Usage:_

"How fast should I cut?"

"Many coaches suggest about 0.5–1% of your bodyweight a week. Much faster makes it harder to keep muscle."
