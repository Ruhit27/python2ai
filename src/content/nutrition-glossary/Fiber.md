description: The part of plant foods the body can't digest. It helps digestion, feeds gut bacteria, and keeps you full.
---

A type of [carbohydrate](./Carbohydrate.md) in plant foods — vegetables, fruit, whole grains, beans, nuts — that human digestion can't fully break down. It passes through the gut mostly intact, providing few [calories](./Calorie.md).

There are two broad kinds. **Soluble** fiber (in oats, beans, apples) absorbs water to form a gel, which slows digestion and can help lower cholesterol. **Insoluble** fiber (in whole grains, vegetable skins) adds bulk and keeps things moving. Many fibers also feed the helpful bacteria in the gut.

Fiber increases [satiety](./Satiety.md), which makes high-fiber foods useful in a [calorie deficit](./Calorie%20deficit.md). Many health guidelines suggest roughly 25–35 g a day for adults, but most people eat far less. Increasing it gradually, with enough [water](./Hydration.md), avoids bloating.

_Avoid:_ "fiber" as only a digestion topic — it also affects fullness, blood sugar, and gut health.

_Usage:_

"I'm always hungry on this diet."

"Add more fiber — beans, oats, vegetables. You'll feel fuller on the same calories."
