A [mineral](./Mineral.md) the body uses to make hemoglobin, the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen. Low iron leads to tiredness, breathlessness, and poorer exercise performance, and eventually to *iron-deficiency anemia*.

Iron deficiency is one of the most common [micronutrient](./Micronutrient.md) deficiencies in the world. People at higher risk include those who menstruate, pregnant people, vegetarians and vegans, frequent blood donors, and endurance athletes.

Food iron comes in two forms. **Heme** iron, in red meat, poultry, and fish, is absorbed well. **Non-heme** iron, in beans, lentils, spinach, and fortified cereals, is absorbed less well — but eating it with [vitamin](./Vitamin.md) C helps, while tea and coffee with meals reduce absorption. Too much iron is also harmful, so [supplements](./Supplement.md) are best taken only after a blood test shows a need.

_Avoid:_ "spinach is packed with iron" — it contains some, but it's poorly absorbed compared with iron from meat.

_Usage:_

"I'm vegetarian and always tired."

"Worth getting your iron checked. Pair beans and lentils with vitamin C to absorb more."
