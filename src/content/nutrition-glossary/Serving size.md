---
description: The standard amount a nutrition label's numbers refer to. Set by the manufacturer or regulations, not by how much you eat.
---

The reference amount that a [nutrition label](./Nutrition%20label.md)'s values are based on — for example, "per 30 g serving" or "per 1 cup". In some countries serving sizes follow official rules; in others the manufacturer chooses them.

The catch is that the serving size may be far smaller than a realistic [portion](./Portion%20size.md). A bag of chips might list "per 25 g serving" while most people eat the whole 150 g bag — six times the calories on the label. Drinks, cereals, and snacks are common examples.

When comparing foods, use the per-100 g values instead. When [tracking](./Tracking%20macros.md), weigh what you actually eat and scale the numbers accordingly.

_Avoid:_ "one package = one serving" — many packages contain several servings.

_Usage:_

"This ice cream is only 140 calories!"

"Per serving — and there are four servings in that tub."
