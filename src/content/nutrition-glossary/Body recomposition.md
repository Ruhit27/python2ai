Losing fat and building muscle at the same time, so body shape changes while weight stays similar. Often shortened to *recomp*. It's most realistic for beginners, people returning after a break, and people with more fat to lose.

From the nutrition side, recomp usually means eating around [maintenance](./Maintenance%20calories.md) or in a small [deficit](./Calorie%20deficit.md), with high [protein](./Protein.md) — research on recomposition often uses around 1.6–2.2 g per kilogram of bodyweight per day (0.7–1 g per lb). Enough protein lets the body build muscle even without a [calorie surplus](./Calorie%20surplus.md), while the slight deficit trims fat.

Because the scale barely moves, progress is best measured by waist size, photos, strength gains, and [body fat](./Body%20fat%20percentage.md) trends rather than weight. For lean, experienced lifters, recomp is very slow, and separate [bulk](./Bulking.md) and [cut](./Cutting.md) phases usually work better. The training side is covered in the Gym Glossary.

_Avoid:_ "you can't lose fat and gain muscle at once" — for many people, especially beginners, you can.

_Usage:_

"Should I bulk or cut first? I'm new and a bit overweight."

"Neither — eat around maintenance, hit your protein, and lift. You'll recomp."
