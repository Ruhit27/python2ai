A [supplement](./Supplement.md) that contains a range of [vitamins](./Vitamin.md) and [minerals](./Mineral.md) in one product, usually around the recommended daily amounts.

A multivitamin can fill gaps for people who can't or don't eat a varied diet: those on very low-calorie plans, some older adults, people with certain medical conditions, or those with restricted diets. For generally healthy people eating well, large studies haven't found that multivitamins prevent heart disease, cancer, or early death.

They also don't replace [whole foods](./Whole%20foods.md), which provide [fiber](./Fiber.md) and many other beneficial compounds not found in pills. Some nutrients are better handled individually when there's a real need — vitamin D in winter, [iron](./Iron.md) for deficiency, B12 for vegans — ideally after a blood test.

_Avoid:_ "a multivitamin makes up for a bad diet" — it adds some micronutrients but none of the rest of what good food provides.

_Usage:_

"I take a multivitamin, so I don't need vegetables, right?"

"The pill doesn't give you fiber or everything else vegetables have. Eat them."
