---
description: Body mass index: weight divided by height squared. A quick screening tool for populations, but a rough measure for individuals.
---

Stands for **B**ody **M**ass **I**ndex: your weight in kilograms divided by your height in metres squared (kg/m²). A person weighing 70 kg (155 lb) who is 1.75 m (5 ft 9 in) tall has a BMI of about 22.9.

Common adult categories: under 18.5 is underweight; 18.5–24.9 is the healthy range; 25–29.9 is overweight; 30 or more is obese. Some populations use lower cut-offs, for example many Asian countries.

BMI is useful for studying large groups and as a quick first check, because it's easy to measure and linked with health risks across populations. But it can't tell muscle from fat: a muscular athlete can be "overweight" by BMI while being lean, and someone with little muscle can be in the "healthy" range with a high [body fat percentage](./Body%20fat%20percentage.md). Waist size and body composition ([lean mass](./Lean%20mass.md) vs fat) add important detail.

_Avoid:_ "BMI measures body fat" — it only uses height and weight, so it can't tell muscle from fat.

_Usage:_

"My BMI says I'm overweight, but I lift a lot."

"BMI can't see muscle. Your waist measurement is a better check."
