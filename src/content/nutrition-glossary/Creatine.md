A natural compound made by the body and found in meat and fish, stored mostly in muscle. It helps muscles regenerate energy quickly for short, intense efforts like heavy lifts or sprints.

As a [supplement](./Supplement.md), *creatine monohydrate* is one of the most researched in sports nutrition, with consistent evidence that it improves strength, power, and training performance, and supports muscle gain alongside training. Vegetarians and vegans, who get little creatine from food, often see bigger effects. Research is also exploring possible benefits for brain health and ageing.

Decades of studies have found it safe for healthy adults at commonly used amounts. It causes a small gain in [water weight](./Water%20weight.md) — about 1–2 kg (2–4 lb) of water stored in the muscle — which is not fat. Worries about kidney damage come mainly from a harmless rise in a blood marker (creatinine); people with existing kidney disease should still ask a doctor first. The training side is covered in the Gym Glossary.

_Avoid:_ "creatine is bad for your kidneys" — in healthy people, research hasn't found harm.

_Usage:_

"Is creatine safe to take long-term?"

"For healthy adults, it's one of the best-studied supplements, and the research says yes."
