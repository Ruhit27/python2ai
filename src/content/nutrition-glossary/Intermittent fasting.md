description: An eating pattern that limits eating to certain hours or days, like 16:8. Works mainly by making it easier to eat less.

An eating pattern that alternates set periods of eating and fasting. Common versions: **16:8** (eating within an 8-hour window each day, such as noon to 8 pm), **5:2** (eating normally five days and very little on two), and alternate-day fasting.

Research shows intermittent fasting helps with weight loss about as well as ordinary calorie restriction when total [calories](./Calorie.md) are the same. Its main benefit is practical: for some people, skipping breakfast or closing the kitchen after dinner is an easy way to create a [calorie deficit](./Calorie%20deficit.md). Claims of special fat-burning effects are mostly overstated.

It suits some people and not others. Those who get very hungry or overeat later may do worse with it. For muscle building, fitting enough [protein](./Protein.md) into a short window is harder — see [meal timing](./Meal%20timing.md). People with a history of disordered eating, pregnant people, and those with diabetes should talk to a doctor before fasting.

_Avoid:_ "fasting burns more fat" — without eating fewer calories overall, it doesn't lead to extra fat loss.

_Usage:_

"Should I try 16:8 to lose weight?"

"If skipping breakfast helps you eat less overall, sure. If it makes you binge at night, skip it."
