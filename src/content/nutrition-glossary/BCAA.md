description: Branched-chain amino acids: three essential amino acids sold as a supplement. Largely unnecessary if you eat enough protein.
---

Stands for **B**ranched-**C**hain **A**mino **A**cids: three of the [essential amino acids](./Essential%20amino%20acids.md) — leucine, isoleucine, and valine — which have a branched chemical structure. They're sold as a [supplement](./Supplement.md), often as flavored drinks sipped during training.

The idea is that BCAAs, especially leucine, trigger muscle building. But building muscle needs all nine essential amino acids, not just three. Research finds that for people eating enough [protein](./Protein.md), BCAAs add little or nothing for muscle growth or recovery.

Whole protein — food or [protein powder](./Protein%20powder.md) — already contains plenty of BCAAs, along with the other [amino acids](./Amino%20acid.md), and usually costs less per gram. BCAAs might be marginally useful when training fasted, but a small protein shake does the same job better.

_Avoid:_ "BCAAs build muscle on their own" — without the other essential amino acids, they can't.

_Usage:_

"Should I sip BCAAs during my workout?"

"If you eat enough protein, it's mostly flavored water. Spend the money on food."
