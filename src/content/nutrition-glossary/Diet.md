description: Two meanings: everything you normally eat, and a planned eating pattern for a goal like weight loss.

The word has two meanings, and they're often confused.

**Diet as your normal eating.** In nutrition science, your diet is simply everything you usually eat and drink — "a Mediterranean diet", "a high-protein diet". Everyone has a diet.

**Diet as a plan.** In everyday speech, "going on a diet" means following a set of rules to change your body, usually to lose weight — keto, [intermittent fasting](./Intermittent%20fasting.md), low-fat, vegan, and so on.

The two connect: a planned diet only works long-term if it becomes your normal diet. Research comparing popular weight-loss diets finds that most work similarly when they produce the same [calorie deficit](./Calorie%20deficit.md), and the best predictor of success is how well people stick to them. Diets that give up whole food groups, like [keto](./Ketosis.md), can work but are harder for many people to maintain.

_Avoid:_ "the best diet" — there isn't one; the best diet is the one you can keep up that meets your needs.

_Usage:_

"Which diet should I go on?"

"Think less 'go on a diet' and more 'what can my normal diet look like for good'?"
