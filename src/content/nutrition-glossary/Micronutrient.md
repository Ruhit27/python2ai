---
description: Vitamins and minerals: nutrients needed in tiny amounts, with no calories, but essential for the body to work.
---

The [vitamins](./Vitamin.md) and [minerals](./Mineral.md) the body needs in small amounts — milligrams or micrograms, not grams. Unlike [macronutrients](./Macronutrient.md), they provide no [calories](./Calorie.md), but they're essential for almost everything: making energy, building bones, carrying oxygen, fighting infection.

Most people get enough from a varied diet rich in [whole foods](./Whole%20foods.md) — vegetables, fruit, whole grains, dairy, meat, fish, legumes. Deficiencies happen with very restricted diets, certain health conditions, pregnancy, or limited sunlight. Common ones worldwide include [iron](./Iron.md), vitamin D, iodine, and vitamin B12 (for vegans).

A [multivitamin](./Multivitamin.md) can fill gaps but doesn't replace good food. Taking very large doses of some micronutrients can be harmful, so "more" isn't better beyond what you need.

_Avoid:_ "micronutrients don't matter if I hit my macros" — calories and macros don't tell you whether you're getting enough vitamins and minerals.

_Usage:_

"I hit my calories and protein every day with shakes and pasta."

"What about vegetables? You could be low on micronutrients."
