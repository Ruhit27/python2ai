A unit of energy. The "calories" on food labels are technically **kilocalories** (kcal) — 1,000 small calories — but everyone just says calories. Some countries also list energy in kilojoules (kJ): 1 kcal is about 4.2 kJ.

Food provides energy through its [macronutrients](./Macronutrient.md): [protein](./Protein.md) and [carbohydrate](./Carbohydrate.md) give about 4 kcal per gram, [fat](./Fat.md) about 9 kcal per gram, and alcohol about 7. A slice of bread with around 15 g of carbs, 3 g of protein, and 1 g of fat has roughly 80 kcal.

Your body uses energy all day — to stay alive ([BMR](./BMR.md)), to move ([NEAT](./NEAT.md)), to exercise, and to digest food ([thermic effect of food](./Thermic%20effect%20of%20food.md)). The total is your [TDEE](./TDEE.md). Eating less than that is a [calorie deficit](./Calorie%20deficit.md) and leads to weight loss; eating more is a [calorie surplus](./Calorie%20surplus.md) and leads to weight gain. Calories aren't everything about health, but they are what decides weight change.

_Avoid:_ "calories are bad" — calories are simply energy; the question is how much you need.

_Usage:_

"Why does a handful of nuts have more calories than a whole apple?"

"Nuts are mostly fat — 9 calories per gram. The apple is mostly water and carbs."
