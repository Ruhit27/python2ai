---
description: When you eat across the day. It matters far less than what and how much you eat in total.
---

When you eat — how many meals, at what times, and in relation to training. Popular advice has made timing seem very important, but research shows total daily intake of [calories](./Calorie.md) and [protein](./Protein.md) matters far more for body composition than timing.

A few timing details do help a little. Spreading protein across three or four meals seems slightly better for muscle building than one or two huge meals. Having some carbohydrates and protein a few hours before training can improve performance. Athletes training twice a day benefit from eating soon after the first session to refuel.

The famous "anabolic window" — the idea that you must eat within 30 minutes after training — is largely a myth for people who've eaten in the hours before. Patterns like [intermittent fasting](./Intermittent%20fasting.md) are timing strategies too; they work mainly by making a [deficit](./Calorie%20deficit.md) easier, not through timing itself.

_Avoid:_ "eating late at night makes you gain weight" — total calories decide weight change, not the clock.

_Usage:_

"Is it bad to eat after 8 pm?"

"Not in itself. If late snacks push you over your calories, that's the issue, not the time."
