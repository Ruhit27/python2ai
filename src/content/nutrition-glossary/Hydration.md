Having enough water in the body for it to work well. Water makes up around 50–60% of body weight and is involved in everything from temperature control to digestion to carrying nutrients.

Needs vary a lot with body size, climate, and activity, so there's no single number — "8 glasses a day" is a rough rule, not science. Food also provides water, often 20% or more of daily intake, and tea and coffee count too. For most healthy people, drinking to thirst and having pale-yellow urine are good practical guides.

Even mild dehydration — losing about 2% of body weight in fluid — can reduce exercise performance and concentration. During long or hot training, replacing [electrolytes](./Electrolyte.md) like [sodium](./Sodium.md) matters as well as water. Hydration also affects [water weight](./Water%20weight.md), which is why daily scale readings bounce around.

_Avoid:_ "coffee dehydrates you" — its mild effect is outweighed by the water in the drink itself for regular drinkers.

_Usage:_

"How much water should I drink?"

"Enough that you're rarely thirsty and your urine is pale. More on hot days and training days."
