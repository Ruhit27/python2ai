[Minerals](./Mineral.md) that carry an electrical charge when dissolved in body fluids. The main ones are [sodium](./Sodium.md), potassium, chloride, magnesium, and calcium. They control nerve signals, muscle contractions (including the heartbeat), and how water moves in and out of cells.

Sweat contains electrolytes, mostly sodium. For typical gym sessions, normal food and water replace them easily. For long, hot, or very sweaty exercise — say, over an hour of endurance work — drinks or tablets with electrolytes can help maintain [hydration](./Hydration.md) and performance.

Drinking huge amounts of plain water during very long events without replacing sodium can, in rare cases, dangerously dilute blood sodium (*hyponatremia*). For everyday life, electrolyte drinks aren't necessary — many are mostly sugar and salt.

_Avoid:_ "I need electrolyte drinks for every workout" — for most gym sessions, water and normal meals are enough.

_Usage:_

"Should I drink a sports drink in the gym?"

"For an hour of lifting, water's fine. Save electrolytes for long, sweaty sessions."
