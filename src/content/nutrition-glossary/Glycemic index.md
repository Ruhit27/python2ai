---
description: A 0–100 score for how quickly a carbohydrate food raises blood sugar, compared with pure glucose at 100.
---

A scale from 0 to 100 that ranks [carbohydrate](./Carbohydrate.md) foods by how quickly they raise blood sugar after eating, compared with pure glucose, which scores 100. White bread and rice score high (around 70 or more); lentils, most fruit, and oats score lower.

What lowers the GI: [fiber](./Fiber.md), [fat](./Fat.md), [protein](./Protein.md), and less processing, all of which slow digestion. That's why a whole apple has a lower GI than apple juice, and why eating carbs as part of a mixed meal blunts the spike.

The GI has limits. It measures a food eaten alone, in a fixed carb amount — not a real meal or a real portion. *Glycemic load* adjusts for portion size and is often more useful. GI matters most for people managing blood sugar, like those with diabetes; for most people, total calories, fiber, and [whole foods](./Whole%20foods.md) matter more than fine GI differences.

_Avoid:_ "high-GI foods make you fat" — weight depends on total calories; GI mostly affects how blood sugar rises.

_Usage:_

"Is watermelon bad because it's high GI?"

"Its GI is high but its carbs per serving are low — so its glycemic load is small."
