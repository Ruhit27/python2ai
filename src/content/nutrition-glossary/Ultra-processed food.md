description: Industrially made foods built mostly from refined ingredients and additives — soft drinks, packaged snacks, instant noodles.
---

Foods made mostly or entirely from industrial ingredients — refined starches, sugars, oils, protein isolates — plus additives like flavorings, emulsifiers, and colorings, usually ready to eat. Examples include soft drinks, packaged snacks, sweets, instant noodles, many breakfast cereals, and reconstituted meats like nuggets. The term comes from the *NOVA* system, which groups foods by how processed they are.

Diets high in ultra-processed food are linked in many studies with weight gain and poorer health. One controlled study found that people ate about 500 more [calories](./Calorie.md) a day on an ultra-processed diet than on an unprocessed one, even when both were matched for available nutrients — possibly because these foods are high in [calorie density](./Calorie%20density.md), low in [fiber](./Fiber.md) and [satiety](./Satiety.md), and easy to eat quickly.

Not all processing is bad: frozen vegetables, canned beans, and plain yogurt are processed but not ultra-processed. The practical takeaway is to build most meals from [whole foods](./Whole%20foods.md), not to fear every package.

_Avoid:_ "processed" and "ultra-processed" as the same — frozen peas are processed; soft drinks are ultra-processed.

_Usage:_

"Is all processed food bad?"

"No — canned beans and frozen veg are fine. It's the ultra-processed stuff that's easy to overeat."
