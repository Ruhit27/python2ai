A planned period of eating in a [calorie surplus](./Calorie%20surplus.md) to support muscle growth, usually alongside hard strength training. Some fat gain comes with it, so bulks are often followed by a [cut](./Cutting.md).

From the nutrition side, the main questions are how big the surplus is and what fills it. A **lean bulk** uses a modest surplus — roughly 5–15% above [maintenance](./Maintenance%20calories.md) — aiming to gain slowly and limit fat. A **dirty bulk** eats anything and everything, which usually adds far more fat than muscle.

A good bulk still hits enough [protein](./Protein.md) every day, gets most calories from [whole foods](./Whole%20foods.md), and adds carbohydrates to fuel training. Tracking bodyweight weekly shows whether the surplus is the right size: gaining too fast means cutting back a little. The training side is covered in the Gym Glossary.

_Avoid:_ "bulking means eating whatever you want" — that's a dirty bulk, and it adds mostly fat.

_Usage:_

"I'm gaining 2 kg (4.5 lb) a month on my bulk."

"That's fast for an experienced lifter — probably a lot of fat. A slightly smaller surplus would slow it down."
