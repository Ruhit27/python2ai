One of the three [macronutrients](./Macronutrient.md), providing about 4 kcal per gram. Carbohydrates ("carbs") range from sugars — like the glucose in honey or the lactose in milk — to starches in bread, rice, and potatoes, to [fiber](./Fiber.md), which the body mostly can't digest.

The body breaks most carbs down into glucose, which fuels the brain and hard exercise. Extra is stored in the muscles and liver as *glycogen* — along with water, which is why cutting carbs causes quick [water weight](./Water%20weight.md) loss. The [glycemic index](./Glycemic%20index.md) describes how quickly a carb raises blood sugar.

Carbs aren't fattening in themselves; weight change depends on total [calories](./Calorie.md). Whole-food sources — fruit, vegetables, whole grains, beans — also bring fiber and [micronutrients](./Micronutrient.md), while [added sugars](./Added%20sugar.md) and refined carbs are easy to overeat. Very low-carb diets like keto push the body into [ketosis](./Ketosis.md).

_Avoid:_ "carbs make you fat" — excess calories do, from any source.

_Usage:_

"I cut carbs and lost 3 kg (6.5 lb) in a week!"

"Most of that is water stored with glycogen. Fat loss is slower, whatever the diet."
