A product taken to add something to your diet — [vitamins](./Vitamin.md), [minerals](./Mineral.md), [protein powder](./Protein%20powder.md), [creatine](./Creatine.md), [caffeine](./Caffeine.md), herbs, and more. They come as pills, powders, drinks, and bars.

Most supplements have little or no evidence of benefit for healthy people eating a varied diet. A small number are well supported for specific purposes: creatine for strength and power, caffeine for performance, protein powder as a convenient protein source, vitamin D where levels are low, and iron or B12 for deficiencies.

In many countries, supplements are regulated much less strictly than medicines: they don't have to prove they work before being sold, and some products have been found to contain less of the ingredient than claimed, or banned substances. Choosing products tested by independent third parties reduces the risk. Supplements can also interact with medications, so it's worth checking with a doctor or pharmacist.

_Avoid:_ "natural means safe" — supplements can have side effects and interactions like any active substance.

_Usage:_

"What supplements should I take as a beginner?"

"Honestly, probably just protein powder if you struggle to hit protein, and maybe creatine. Food first."
