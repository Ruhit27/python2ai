All the chemical processes in the body that keep you alive — including breaking down food for energy and building and repairing tissue. In everyday talk, "metabolism" usually means how many [calories](./Calorie.md) your body burns in a day, your [TDEE](./TDEE.md).

People differ, but less than they think. Most of the difference in resting energy use ([BMR](./BMR.md)) is explained by body size and [lean mass](./Lean%20mass.md): bigger and more muscular bodies burn more. The biggest *variable* part between people is day-to-day movement ([NEAT](./NEAT.md)), not some hidden "fast" or "slow" metabolism.

Metabolism does adapt during a long [calorie deficit](./Calorie%20deficit.md): as you lose weight, you burn less (a smaller body needs less energy), and the body also cuts back a little further and tends to move less. This *adaptive thermogenesis* is real but modest — it slows fat loss; it doesn't stop it or cause weight gain.

_Avoid:_ "starvation mode" — the body does save some energy in a deficit, but it never stops fat loss while you stay in a true deficit.

_Usage:_

"I think my metabolism is just slow."

"Maybe a little — but check your daily steps first. Movement varies far more between people than metabolism does."
