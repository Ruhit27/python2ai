Stands for **T**otal **D**aily **E**nergy **E**xpenditure: every [calorie](./Calorie.md) your body uses in a day. It adds up four parts:

| Part | What it is | Typical share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [BMR](./BMR.md) | Staying alive at rest | 60–70% |
| [NEAT](./NEAT.md) | Everyday movement: walking, fidgeting, chores | 10–30% |
| Exercise | Planned workouts | 5–10% |
| [Thermic effect of food](./Thermic%20effect%20of%20food.md) | Digesting food | ~10% |

If you eat about your TDEE, your weight stays roughly stable — that's [maintenance](./Maintenance%20calories.md). Eating below it creates a [calorie deficit](./Calorie%20deficit.md); above it, a [calorie surplus](./Calorie%20surplus.md).

Online calculators estimate TDEE, but they can easily be off by a few hundred calories. The most reliable way is to track what you eat and your bodyweight for two or three weeks: if weight stays steady, your average intake is your TDEE.

_Avoid:_ "calories burned" from a fitness watch as your TDEE — wearables often overestimate exercise calories.

_Usage:_

"The calculator says my TDEE is 2,600, but I'm gaining on 2,400."

"Then your real TDEE is lower. Trust the scale over the calculator."
