Everything in the body that isn't fat: muscle, bone, organs, skin, and the water within them. If you weigh 80 kg (175 lb) at 20% [body fat](./Body%20fat%20percentage.md), your lean mass is 64 kg (140 lb). The term *fat-free mass* means almost the same thing.

In fitness talk, "lean mass" is often used loosely to mean muscle. That's close but not exact: lean mass also changes with [water](./Water%20weight.md) and glycogen stored in muscle, so it can jump up or down quickly without any real muscle change — for example when starting [creatine](./Creatine.md) or cutting carbs.

Keeping lean mass during a [cut](./Cutting.md) is a key goal, and gaining it is the goal of a [bulk](./Bulking.md). Lean mass is also the main factor behind [BMR](./BMR.md): more lean mass means a higher resting calorie burn.

_Avoid:_ "lean mass" as pure muscle — it includes bone, organs, and water too.

_Usage:_

"The scan says I gained 2 kg (4.5 lb) of lean mass in a month!"

"Some of that's probably water and glycogen. Real muscle gain is slower."
