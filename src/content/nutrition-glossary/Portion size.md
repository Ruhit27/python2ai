The amount of a food you actually eat at one time — whatever you put on your plate. It often differs from the [serving size](./Serving%20size.md) listed on a package, which is a standard reference amount.

Portion sizes shape [calorie](./Calorie.md) intake more than people realize. Restaurant meals and packaged snacks have grown larger over the decades, and people tend to eat what's in front of them. Research shows larger plates and packages lead people to eat more without noticing.

Simple ways to manage portions without [tracking](./Tracking%20macros.md): the "hand method" (a palm of protein, a fist of vegetables, a cupped hand of carbs, a thumb of fats per meal), smaller plates, filling half the plate with vegetables, and serving food rather than eating from the package. Foods with low [calorie density](./Calorie%20density.md) allow bigger portions for the same calories.

_Avoid:_ "portion" and "serving" as the same — a portion is what you eat; a serving is the label's reference amount.

_Usage:_

"The cereal box says 150 calories per serving."

"But your bowl is probably three servings. Check your portion against the label."
