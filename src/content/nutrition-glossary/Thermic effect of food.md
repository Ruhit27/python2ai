The energy the body spends digesting, absorbing, and processing the food you eat. It's often shortened to *TEF* and makes up roughly 10% of total daily energy use ([TDEE](./TDEE.md)).

It differs by [macronutrient](./Macronutrient.md). [Protein](./Protein.md) has the highest effect: about 20–30% of its calories are used just to process it. [Carbohydrates](./Carbohydrate.md) take about 5–10%, and [fat](./Fat.md) only 0–3%. So 100 kcal of protein gives the body fewer usable calories than 100 kcal of fat.

This is one reason high-protein diets help with fat loss, alongside protein's strong effect on [satiety](./Satiety.md). Otherwise, TEF is small and hard to change much: eating more, smaller meals doesn't "stoke the metabolism", because total TEF depends on how much you eat, not how often.

_Avoid:_ "eating six small meals boosts metabolism" — the thermic effect depends on the total food, not the number of meals.

_Usage:_

"Should I eat every three hours to keep my metabolism high?"

"No — three meals or six burn the same for digestion. Eat on whatever schedule suits you."
