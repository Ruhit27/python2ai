Recording the grams of each [macronutrient](./Macronutrient.md) — [protein](./Protein.md), [carbohydrate](./Carbohydrate.md), and [fat](./Fat.md) — you eat each day, usually with an app, to hit targets set for a goal. Because macros add up to [calories](./Calorie.md), tracking macros also tracks calories. People often call it *IIFYM* ("if it fits your macros") when it's done flexibly.

Tracking is the most precise way to control intake. It teaches what portions actually look like, reveals hidden calories (oils, drinks, sauces), and makes it easy to adjust — for example, raising protein during a [cut](./Cutting.md). Weighing food with a kitchen scale is much more accurate than estimating [portions](./Portion%20size.md) by eye.

Its downsides: it takes time, food labels and app entries can be off by 10–20%, and for some people constant tracking becomes stressful. Many people track for a few months to learn, then switch to eating by habit.

_Avoid:_ "tracking" as dieting — it's just measuring; the targets decide whether you lose, maintain, or gain.

_Usage:_

"I think I eat 2,000 calories, but I'm not losing weight."

"Track everything for a week, including cooking oil and drinks. It's usually more than people think."
