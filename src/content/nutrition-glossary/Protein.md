description: A macronutrient made of amino acids, used to build and repair every tissue. From the nutrition side: where to get it and how much.
---

One of the three [macronutrients](./Macronutrient.md), providing about 4 kcal per gram. Proteins are made of chains of [amino acids](./Amino%20acid.md), which the body uses to build and repair muscle, skin, organs, enzymes, and hormones.

Good sources include meat, fish, eggs, dairy, tofu, tempeh, legumes, and [protein powder](./Protein%20powder.md). Animal proteins and soy contain all the [essential amino acids](./Essential%20amino%20acids.md) in good proportions; most other plant proteins are lower in one or more, but eating a variety of them covers it.

The basic health guideline is about 0.8 g per kilogram of bodyweight per day (about 0.36 g per lb). Research on people who lift weights finds more helps build muscle, up to roughly 1.6 g/kg (0.7 g/lb) a day. Protein is also the most filling macro ([satiety](./Satiety.md)) and has the highest [thermic effect](./Thermic%20effect%20of%20food.md), which is why higher-protein diets help during a [cut](./Cutting.md). For healthy people, high protein intakes haven't been shown to harm the kidneys.

_Avoid:_ "you can only absorb 30 g of protein per meal" — the body absorbs it all; larger meals are just used over a longer time.

_Usage:_

"How do vegetarians get enough protein?"

"Eggs, dairy, tofu, lentils, beans — and a mix of them covers all the essential amino acids."
