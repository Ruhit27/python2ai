Stands for **B**asal **M**etabolic **R**ate: the number of [calories](./Calorie.md) your body burns at complete rest to keep you alive — breathing, pumping blood, keeping warm, running your organs. It's what you'd burn lying in bed all day doing nothing.

BMR is usually the largest part of your daily energy use, often about 60–70% of [TDEE](./TDEE.md) for fairly inactive people. It's mainly set by body size, [lean mass](./Lean%20mass.md), age, and sex. A person weighing 80 kg (175 lb) might have a BMR around 1,700 kcal; a smaller person, closer to 1,300.

BMR is estimated with formulas based on height, weight, age, and sex, and those estimates are usually within about 10% for most people. Building muscle raises it slightly, but less than often claimed — each kilogram of muscle burns roughly 13 kcal a day at rest (about 6 per lb).

_Avoid:_ "muscle burns huge amounts of calories" — muscle raises BMR a little, but the effect is small per kilogram.

_Usage:_

"How many calories do I burn if I do nothing?"

"That's your BMR — a calculator can estimate it from your height, weight, and age. Add movement and exercise to get your real total."
