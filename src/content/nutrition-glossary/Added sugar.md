Sugars and syrups added to foods and drinks during processing, cooking, or at the table — table sugar, honey, syrups, fruit-juice concentrates — as opposed to the sugars naturally present in whole fruit, vegetables, and milk. Some labels show "added sugars" separately; others show only total sugars.

Health bodies recommend limiting added sugars. The World Health Organization advises keeping "free sugars" (added sugars plus those in honey, syrups, and fruit juice) under 10% of daily [calories](./Calorie.md), ideally under 5%. On a 2,000-kcal diet, 10% is about 50 g — roughly the amount in one large can of soft drink.

Added sugar provides calories without [fiber](./Fiber.md), [protein](./Protein.md), or [micronutrients](./Micronutrient.md), and is low in [satiety](./Satiety.md) — especially in drinks, which don't make you feel full. It's also linked with tooth decay. Sugar isn't uniquely fattening beyond its calories, but it makes overeating easy.

_Avoid:_ "fruit sugar is as bad as added sugar" — whole fruit comes with fiber and water that slow eating and digestion.

_Usage:_

"Is honey healthier than sugar?"

"Not really — both count as added sugar. The body handles them almost the same."
