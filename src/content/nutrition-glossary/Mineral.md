An inorganic element the body needs from food, in small amounts. Minerals are [micronutrients](./Micronutrient.md), alongside [vitamins](./Vitamin.md).

Some are needed in relatively larger amounts (*major minerals*), like calcium for bones, potassium and [sodium](./Sodium.md) for nerves and fluid balance, and magnesium for muscles and energy. Others are needed only in traces (*trace minerals*), like [iron](./Iron.md) for carrying oxygen, zinc for immunity, and iodine for the thyroid.

Several minerals act as [electrolytes](./Electrolyte.md), carrying electrical charges that control muscle contractions and fluid balance. Dairy, leafy greens, nuts, seeds, meat, fish, and whole grains are good sources. Too little and too much can both cause problems, so large-dose single-mineral [supplements](./Supplement.md) are best taken only when there's a known need.

_Avoid:_ "minerals" as only calcium — there are many, each with different jobs.

_Usage:_

"Why do I get muscle cramps?"

"Could be many things — sometimes low magnesium or electrolytes, but often just fatigue."
