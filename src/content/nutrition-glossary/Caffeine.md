A natural stimulant found in coffee, tea, cocoa, and many energy drinks and [pre-workout](./Pre-workout.md) products. It works mainly by blocking a brain chemical (adenosine) that builds up to make you feel tired.

Caffeine is one of the few [supplements](./Supplement.md) with strong evidence for improving exercise performance: endurance, strength, and power all tend to improve modestly, and effort feels easier. Health authorities generally consider up to about 400 mg a day safe for most healthy adults — roughly four cups of brewed coffee — with lower limits during pregnancy.

It has a half-life of about 5 hours, so an afternoon coffee can still affect sleep quality at night — and poor sleep hurts training and recovery far more than caffeine helps. People vary a lot in sensitivity, and regular users build some tolerance. Too much can cause jitters, anxiety, a racing heart, and stomach upset.

_Avoid:_ "caffeine dehydrates you" — for regular drinkers, the water in coffee more than makes up for the mild effect.

_Usage:_

"I take a pre-workout at 7 pm and can't sleep."

"It's the caffeine — it lasts for hours. Train earlier or use a caffeine-free version."
