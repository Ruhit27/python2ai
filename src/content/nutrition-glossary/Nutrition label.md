The information panel on packaged food that shows its nutritional content. The exact format varies by country, but most show [energy](./Calorie.md) (kcal and/or kJ), [fat](./Fat.md) (often with saturated fat), [carbohydrates](./Carbohydrate.md) (often with sugars), [fiber](./Fiber.md), [protein](./Protein.md), and [salt or sodium](./Sodium.md).

Labels give values per [serving](./Serving%20size.md), per 100 g (or 100 ml), or both. The per-100 g column is the easiest way to compare two products fairly, since manufacturers choose their own serving sizes. Some countries add front-of-pack color codes or ratings to show at a glance whether a product is high in fat, sugar, or salt.

Reading labels well means checking the serving size against what you'll actually eat, looking at [added sugars](./Added%20sugar.md) and sodium, and glancing at the ingredients list, which is ordered by weight — the first few ingredients make up most of the product.

_Avoid:_ "low fat" on the front as the whole story — check the label; low-fat products sometimes have more sugar.

_Usage:_

"This granola says it's healthy."

"Flip it over — per 100 g it's 25% sugar. The label tells the real story."
