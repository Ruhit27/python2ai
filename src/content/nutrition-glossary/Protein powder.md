A powdered [supplement](./Supplement.md) made by extracting [protein](./Protein.md) from food. Common types:

| Type | Source | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whey | Milk | Digests quickly; high in leucine |
| Casein | Milk | Digests slowly |
| Soy, pea, rice | Plants | Good for vegans; blends balance [amino acids](./Essential%20amino%20acids.md) |

A scoop typically provides 20–25 g of protein and around 100–130 [calories](./Calorie.md). Nutritionally, it's simply food protein in a convenient form: it helps people who struggle to eat enough protein from meals, especially during a [cut](./Cutting.md) or on a plant-based diet.

It isn't required for building muscle — total daily protein matters, not where it comes from. Quality varies between brands, so products tested by independent third parties are a safer choice. Whey contains lactose, so people who are lactose intolerant often do better with whey *isolate* or plant powders.

_Avoid:_ "protein shakes build muscle" — training builds muscle; the shake is just protein, like a chicken breast.

_Usage:_

"Do I need protein powder to build muscle?"

"No, but it's handy. If you hit your protein from food, skip it."
