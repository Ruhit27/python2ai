Taking in more [calories](./Calorie.md) than you burn ([TDEE](./TDEE.md)). The extra energy has to go somewhere: some is stored as body fat, and — if you're doing resistance training and eating enough [protein](./Protein.md) — some supports building new muscle.

A surplus is the basis of [bulking](./Bulking.md). Research suggests a modest surplus, roughly 5–15% above [maintenance](./Maintenance%20calories.md) (often 200–400 kcal a day), is enough to support muscle growth for most people; larger surpluses mostly add fat faster, not muscle.

Without training, a surplus just leads to fat gain. And beginners often don't need a surplus to build muscle at all — see [body recomposition](./Body%20recomposition.md).

_Avoid:_ "eat big to get big" — past a modest surplus, extra food mostly becomes fat.

_Usage:_

"I'm eating 1,000 calories over maintenance to grow faster."

"You'll gain weight faster, but most of it will be fat. A surplus of a few hundred is usually enough."
