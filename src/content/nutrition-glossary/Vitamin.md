An organic compound the body needs in small amounts and mostly can't make itself, so it must come from food. There are 13, grouped into two kinds by how they're absorbed and stored:

| Kind | Vitamins | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fat-soluble | A, D, E, K | Stored in the body; absorbed with [fat](./Fat.md); too much can build up |
| Water-soluble | B vitamins, C | Not stored much; excess mostly leaves in urine |

Each has specific jobs: vitamin C in healing and immunity, vitamin D in bone health, B12 in nerves and red blood cells. Vitamin D is unusual: the skin makes it in sunlight, so deficiency is common in winter and at higher latitudes.

Vitamins are [micronutrients](./Micronutrient.md), alongside [minerals](./Mineral.md). A varied diet covers most needs; [multivitamins](./Multivitamin.md) and single-vitamin [supplements](./Supplement.md) help in specific cases, like vitamin D in winter or B12 on a vegan diet.

_Avoid:_ "extra vitamin C prevents colds" — large doses have at most a small effect on how long colds last.

_Usage:_

"Should I take vitamin D?"

"In winter, lots of people are low. It's worth asking your doctor for a blood test."
