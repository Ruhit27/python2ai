---
description: The building blocks of protein. The body links about 20 kinds together in different orders to make every protein it needs.
---

The small molecules that link together in chains to form [proteins](./Protein.md). The body uses about 20 different amino acids; the order they're joined in decides what each protein is — a muscle fiber, an enzyme, a hormone.

When you eat protein, digestion breaks it down into amino acids, which are absorbed and reassembled into whatever proteins the body needs. Nine of them are [essential](./Essential%20amino%20acids.md): the body can't make them, so they must come from food. The rest can be made from other nutrients.

Some amino acids are sold separately as [supplements](./Supplement.md), such as [BCAAs](./BCAA.md) (three essential amino acids) or glutamine. For most people eating enough protein, these add little, because whole protein already contains all of them.

_Avoid:_ "amino acids" as something separate from protein — protein is made of amino acids.

_Usage:_

"Do I need amino acid supplements?"

"Not if you eat enough protein — it's made of amino acids already."
