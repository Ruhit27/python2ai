Taking in fewer [calories](./Calorie.md) than you burn ([TDEE](./TDEE.md)). The body covers the gap by drawing on stored energy, mostly body fat — which is how weight loss works, whatever the diet is called.

A deficit of about 500 kcal a day is a common starting point; it often leads to losing roughly 0.5 kg (1 lb) a week at first. The old rule that 3,500 kcal equals one pound of fat is a rough approximation — real weight loss slows over time as [maintenance](./Maintenance%20calories.md) falls and [metabolism](./Metabolism.md) adapts.

Bigger deficits lose weight faster but make hunger, energy, and muscle loss worse. Eating plenty of [protein](./Protein.md) and high-[satiety](./Satiety.md) foods, and continuing strength training, helps make sure the weight lost is mostly fat rather than [lean mass](./Lean%20mass.md). A deliberate, planned deficit for fat loss is called a [cut](./Cutting.md).

_Avoid:_ "eat less, move more" as the whole story — it's true, but hunger, sleep, and food choices decide how easy it is to stick to.

_Usage:_

"Which diet is best for fat loss?"

"Whichever one lets you hold a calorie deficit without being miserable. The deficit does the work."
