Money a business spends to run itself and to produce what it sells. [Revenue](./Revenue.md) minus costs is [profit](./Profit.md).

Costs come in two broad kinds. **Variable costs** rise and fall with each sale: for a coffee shop, the beans, milk, and cup in every coffee. **Fixed costs** stay the same whether you sell ten coffees or ten thousand: rent, insurance, a manager's salary. The split matters because it decides your [break-even](./Break-even.md) point — how much you have to sell before fixed costs are covered.

Accountants also separate the cost of making the product itself (often called *cost of goods sold*, or COGS) from the cost of running the company (marketing, office, admin — often called *operating expenses*). Revenue minus COGS gives gross [margin](./Margin.md); take away operating expenses too and you get close to operating profit.

_Avoid:_ "expense" is a near-synonym and fine; avoid "spend" in formal reports, where it is ambiguous about timing.

_Usage:_

"Why does our profit drop when we get busier?"

"Our variable costs are high — every extra order costs us nearly as much as it earns."
