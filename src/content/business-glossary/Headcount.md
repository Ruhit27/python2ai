description: The number of people a company employs. Also each approved position in the plan.
---

The number of people employed by a company or team. "We have a headcount of 120" means 120 employees.

In planning, headcount also means approved positions: "we have two headcount for next quarter" means the [budget](./Budget.md) allows two new hires, even though no one is in those roles yet. Because salaries, benefits, and equipment usually make up the largest share of [costs](./Cost.md) in service and software companies, headcount is one of the most carefully controlled parts of a budget.

Headcount changes are slow and expensive to reverse — hiring takes months and cutting staff is costly and painful — so companies often use contractors or [outsourcing](./Outsourcing.md) for work they're unsure they'll need long-term. For a startup, hiring is usually the biggest driver of [burn rate](./Burn%20rate.md).

_Avoid:_ "resources" when you mean people — say headcount or name the roles.

_Usage:_

"Can I hire a designer?"

"There's no headcount for it this half. Could a contractor cover it until the next budget?"
