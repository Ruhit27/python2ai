---
description: The percentage of people who take a desired next step, such as visitors who sign up or trials that pay.
---

The percentage of people who take a specific desired action out of all who had the chance. If 2,000 people visit a pricing page and 60 start a subscription, the conversion rate is 3%.

Conversion rates are measured at each stage of the [funnel](./Funnel.md): visitor to sign-up, sign-up to trial, trial to paid, and so on. They're useful because they show where the drop-off is. A company with plenty of traffic but a 0.5% sign-up rate has a different problem from one with few visitors who mostly buy.

Small improvements multiply. Raising conversion from 2% to 3% brings in 50% more customers from the same traffic — which directly lowers [CAC](./CAC.md). Common levers include clearer [value propositions](./Value%20proposition.md), simpler checkout, better-targeted [leads](./Lead.md), and [pricing](./Pricing.md) changes.

_Avoid:_ "sales" — conversion is a rate, not a count. 100 sales could be a great or terrible conversion rate depending on the traffic.

_Usage:_

"We got 300 new customers from the campaign!"

"From 100,000 clicks, though. That's a 0.3% conversion rate — worse than our usual."
