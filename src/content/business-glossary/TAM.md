description: Total addressable market: the total revenue available if you sold to every possible customer. The ceiling on size.
---

Stands for **T**otal **A**ddressable **M**arket: the total [revenue](./Revenue.md) a product could earn if every possible customer bought it. It sets the upper limit on how big the business could become.

It's usually estimated either top-down (a research report says "the market for X is $5 billion") or, more convincingly, bottom-up: number of potential customers × what each would pay per year. 200,000 dental clinics × $1,200 a year = a $240 million TAM for dental booking software.

TAM is often narrowed further: **SAM** (serviceable addressable market) is the part you can realistically reach with your product and channels, and **SOM** (serviceable obtainable market) is the part you might actually win. [Venture capital](./Venture%20capital.md) investors pay close attention to TAM, because even a dominant [market share](./Market%20share.md) of a small market won't produce the huge returns they need.

_Avoid:_ "market share" — TAM is the size of the whole pie; market share is your slice of it.

_Usage:_

"Investors say our market is too small."

"Our TAM is $40 million. Even at 30% share, that's a nice business, but not a venture-scale one."
