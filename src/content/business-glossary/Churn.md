description: The rate at which customers stop buying or cancel over a period. The leak in the bucket.
---

The rate at which customers leave — cancel a subscription, stop ordering, or move to a competitor — over a set period. If a company starts the month with 1,000 subscribers and 40 cancel, monthly churn is 4%.

Churn quietly limits growth. At 4% a month, a company loses roughly 40% of its customers over a year and has to replace them before it can grow at all. It also lowers [LTV](./LTV.md), because customers who leave sooner are worth less, which makes a high [CAC](./CAC.md) harder to justify.

Businesses sometimes measure **revenue churn** separately from customer churn, because losing one big customer can hurt more than losing ten small ones. High churn usually points to a product problem — the [value proposition](./Value%20proposition.md) doesn't hold up after purchase, or the company lacks [product-market fit](./Product-market%20fit.md) — more than a marketing one.

_Avoid:_ "attrition" is a synonym and fine; don't use "churn" for staff leaving without saying so, since it usually means customers.

_Usage:_

"We added 500 customers this quarter but revenue barely moved."

"We lost 420. Our churn is eating all our new sales."
