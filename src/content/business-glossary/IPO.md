---
description: Initial public offering. When a private company first sells shares on a public stock exchange.
---

Stands for **I**nitial **P**ublic **O**ffering: the first time a private company sells its shares to the general public on a stock exchange. After an IPO, anyone can buy and sell its shares, and the company is "public" or "listed".

Companies go public to raise large amounts of money and to let early [shareholders](./Shareholder.md) — founders, employees, [venture capital](./Venture%20capital.md) investors — sell their shares. The IPO sets a public [valuation](./Valuation.md) that changes every day with the share price.

Being public comes with obligations: publishing its financial statements (the [income statement](./Income%20statement.md), [balance sheet](./Balance%20sheet.md), and [cash flow statement](./Cash%20flow%20statement.md)) every quarter or half-year, depending on the exchange, plus strict rules on what can be said and pressure to hit [forecasts](./Forecast.md). Many companies now stay private longer, or are acquired instead of going public.

_Avoid:_ "going public" is a synonym and fine; "listing" is close but also covers other ways of reaching an exchange.

_Usage:_

"When can I sell my stock options?"

"Realistically after an IPO or an acquisition. Until then there's no market for them."
