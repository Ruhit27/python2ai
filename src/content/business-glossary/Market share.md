---
description: The percentage of all sales in a market that one company captures.
---

The portion of all sales in a market that one company makes, usually as a percentage. If people spend $10 million a year on coffee in a town and one café takes $1.5 million of it, that café has 15% market share.

Market share shows how a company is doing against its competitors, not just against its own past. Growing [revenue](./Revenue.md) by 10% sounds good, but if the whole market grew 30%, market share fell. High market share can bring [economies of scale](./Economies%20of%20scale.md) and pricing power, both sources of [competitive advantage](./Competitive%20advantage.md).

It depends entirely on how the market is defined. A company can have 2% of "coffee" but 40% of "specialty cold brew in the Northeast". Relatedly, [TAM](./TAM.md) measures the size of the whole market, while market share measures your slice of it.

_Avoid:_ "growth" — you can grow and still lose market share if competitors grow faster.

_Usage:_

"Our sales are up 12% this year."

"The category grew 25%, though. We're losing market share."
