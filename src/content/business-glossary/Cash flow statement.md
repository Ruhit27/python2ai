description: The report that shows where cash came from and where it went over a period, split into operations, investing, and financing.
---

A financial report that tracks the actual cash coming into and going out of a business over a period. It explains why the bank balance changed, which the [income statement](./Income%20statement.md) can't do because it follows [accrual](./Accrual.md) rules rather than cash.

It is split into three parts:

| Part | What it covers | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operating | Cash from day-to-day business | Customer payments, salaries |
| Investing | Buying or selling long-term assets | A new espresso machine |
| Financing | Money from or to owners and lenders | A bank loan, a [dividend](./Dividend.md) |

Many analysts read this statement first, because [cash](./Cash%20flow.md) is harder to dress up than [profit](./Profit.md). A company whose profits never turn into operating cash is a warning sign.

_Avoid:_ "cash flow" alone — that is the thing being measured; this is the report that measures it.

_Usage:_

"Our profit went up but the bank balance went down. Why?"

"Check the cash flow statement — we spent $40k on new equipment. That's in investing."
