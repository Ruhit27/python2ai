---
description: Ownership of a company. Also the balance-sheet value left for owners once all debts are paid.
---

The word has two linked meanings.

**Equity as ownership.** Equity is a share of ownership in a company. Founders start with 100% of the equity. When they raise money from [investors](./Venture%20capital.md), they sell some of it — "we gave up 20% equity for $2 million". Employees may get equity as part of their pay, through shares or stock options. Holders of equity are [shareholders](./Shareholder.md).

**Equity on the balance sheet.** On the [balance sheet](./Balance%20sheet.md), equity is what's left for the owners after all [liabilities](./Liability.md) are subtracted from [assets](./Asset.md): *assets − liabilities = equity*. It grows when the company keeps profits and when owners put in money.

The two meanings connect: the balance-sheet figure is the accounting value of everything the owners own. The market often values that ownership very differently — see [valuation](./Valuation.md) — because it prices in future growth that isn't on the balance sheet.

_Avoid:_ "stock" and "shares" name the units of equity; "equity" is the ownership itself. Also not the same as "equality" or fairness.

_Usage:_

"The offer is $120k salary plus 0.5% equity."

"Ask what the latest valuation is — 0.5% of a $10 million company is very different from 0.5% of $500 million."
