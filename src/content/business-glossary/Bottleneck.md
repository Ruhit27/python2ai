---
description: The single slowest step in a process, which limits how fast the whole process can go.
---

The step in a process with the least capacity, which limits the output of the whole thing — like the narrow neck of a bottle limiting how fast water pours out. A coffee shop with three cashiers but one espresso machine can't serve drinks faster than that machine can make them.

The key point is that improving any step other than the bottleneck doesn't help. Adding a fourth cashier just makes the queue for coffee longer. To speed up the whole process, find the bottleneck and either expand it (a second espresso machine), make sure it's never idle, or reduce the work reaching it.

Bottlenecks move. Fix one and another step becomes the slowest. They also appear outside production — a manager who must approve every decision, or a single [supply chain](./Supply%20chain.md) partner, can become the bottleneck. They lengthen [lead time](./Lead%20time.md) and are a common reason [outsourcing](./Outsourcing.md) is considered.

_Avoid:_ "problem" in general — a bottleneck specifically limits throughput because of capacity.

_Usage:_

"Let's hire more developers to ship faster."

"The bottleneck is code review — one person approves everything. More developers will just make that queue longer."
