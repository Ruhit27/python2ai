---
description: How long it takes from starting a process to finishing it — for example, from placing an order to receiving it.
---

The time between starting a process and finishing it. In purchasing, it's how long from placing an order with a supplier to receiving the goods. In production, it's from starting to make something to having it ready. For customers, it's from ordering to delivery.

Lead time decides how far ahead a business has to plan. If new cups take three weeks to arrive, the coffee shop has to reorder when it still has three weeks of cups left — so longer lead times mean holding more [inventory](./Inventory.md) as a buffer. A long lead time anywhere in the [supply chain](./Supply%20chain.md) slows the whole thing.

Shortening lead times makes a business more responsive and frees up [working capital](./Working%20capital.md). Often the biggest delays come from a single [bottleneck](./Bottleneck.md) step rather than the whole process. Lead times also feed into [SLAs](./SLA.md), where a business promises customers a specific delivery time.

_Avoid:_ "lead" (a potential customer) — same spelling, unrelated meaning. Lead time is pronounced "leed".

_Usage:_

"Can we promise the client delivery in two weeks?"

"Our lead time on the parts alone is three weeks. Four weeks is the earliest honest promise."
