---
description: Anything a business owes to someone else: loans, unpaid supplier bills, wages due, taxes due.
---

Anything a business owes to someone else and will have to pay later. Loans are the obvious example, but liabilities also include supplier bills not yet paid, wages earned but not yet paid out, taxes due, and money customers have paid in advance for things not yet delivered.

Like [assets](./Asset.md), liabilities are split by timing: **current liabilities** are due within a year, **long-term liabilities** later, like a five-year bank loan. Borrowed money in particular is called [debt](./Debt.md).

Liabilities sit on the [balance sheet](./Balance%20sheet.md). Assets minus liabilities gives [equity](./Equity.md), the owners' share. Comparing current assets to current liabilities is a quick check of whether a business can pay its bills — its [working capital](./Working%20capital.md).

_Avoid:_ "debt" as a full synonym — all debt is a liability, but not every liability is debt (an unpaid supplier bill is not a loan).

_Usage:_

"We have $200k in the bank, so we're fine."

"We also have $180k in liabilities due this quarter. We're less fine than it looks."
