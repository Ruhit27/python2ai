description: Paying another company to do work you could do in-house, like manufacturing, support, or payroll.
---

Hiring an outside company to do work that could be done internally. Businesses commonly outsource manufacturing, customer support, cleaning, payroll, IT, and software development. Doing the work inside the company is called *in-house*.

The usual reasons are cost (the specialist has [economies of scale](./Economies%20of%20scale.md) or lower wages), expertise (they do it better), and flexibility (paying per job instead of keeping permanent [headcount](./Headcount.md)). It also lets a company focus on the few things that give it a [competitive advantage](./Competitive%20advantage.md) and buy everything else.

The trade-off is control. Outsourced work can be harder to manage, slower to change, and dependent on the partner — it adds a link to the [supply chain](./Supply%20chain.md) that can fail. Contracts usually include an [SLA](./SLA.md) to pin down the quality and speed expected. A rule of thumb: outsource what's necessary but not distinctive; keep in-house what makes you different.

_Avoid:_ "offshoring" as a synonym — offshoring means moving work to another country, whether outsourced or done by your own staff there.

_Usage:_

"Should we build our own payroll system?"

"No — payroll isn't what makes us special. Outsource it and put the team on the product."
