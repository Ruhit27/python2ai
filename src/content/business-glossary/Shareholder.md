---
description: A person or organization that owns part of a company through its shares. Also called a stockholder.
---

A person or organization that owns one or more shares in a company, and so owns part of it. If a company has 1,000,000 shares and you hold 50,000, you own 5%.

Owning shares usually gives three things: a claim on part of the company's value (its [equity](./Equity.md)), a vote on big decisions like electing the board, and sometimes a share of profits paid out as a [dividend](./Dividend.md). Shareholders can be founders, employees with stock options, [venture capital](./Venture%20capital.md) funds, or — after an [IPO](./IPO.md) — anyone who buys the shares on a stock exchange.

Shareholders are one group of [stakeholders](./Stakeholder.md), and their interests don't always match others'. Cutting staff may raise short-term profit for shareholders while hurting employees and customers.

_Avoid:_ "stakeholder" as a synonym — a stakeholder is anyone affected by the business; a shareholder specifically owns shares.

_Usage:_

"Who gets a say in selling the company?"

"The shareholders vote. The founders hold 60%, so in practice it's their call."
