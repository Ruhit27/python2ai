description: Lifetime value: the total profit you expect from one customer over the whole time they stay with you.
---

Stands for **L**ife**t**ime **V**alue (also CLV, customer lifetime value): the total [profit](./Profit.md) — or sometimes [revenue](./Revenue.md) — a business expects to earn from one customer over the whole time they stay a customer.

A simple subscription example: a customer pays $50 a month, the gross [margin](./Margin.md) is 80% (so $40 profit a month), and the average customer stays 25 months before cancelling. LTV is $40 × 25 = $1,000. The time customers stay is set by [churn](./Churn.md): the higher the churn, the shorter the lifetime and the lower the LTV.

LTV is mostly used next to [CAC](./CAC.md). If it costs $300 to win a customer worth $1,000, each new customer is a good investment and it makes sense to spend more on growth. If LTV is lower than CAC, every sale loses money, and growing faster just loses money faster. Together, LTV and CAC are the core of a business's unit economics.

_Avoid:_ "revenue per customer" per month — LTV covers the whole relationship, not one month.

_Usage:_

"Can we afford to pay $600 to acquire a customer?"

"LTV is about $1,800, so yes — that's a 3:1 ratio."
