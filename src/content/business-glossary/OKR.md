---
description: Objectives and key results: a goal-setting method pairing an ambitious aim with a few measurable results.
---

Stands for **O**bjectives and **K**ey **R**esults: a way of setting goals, usually per quarter. The **objective** is a clear, ambitious aim; the **key results** are two to five measurable outcomes that show whether it was achieved.

Example — Objective: *Make new customers successful in their first month.* Key results: raise the 30-day activation rate from 40% to 60%; cut first-month [churn](./Churn.md) from 8% to 5%; get average onboarding time under 15 minutes.

OKRs connect day-to-day work to company goals and are often set at company, team, and sometimes individual level, so each team can see how its work fits the bigger picture. They are commonly built around [KPIs](./KPI.md) — the key results often aim to move a KPI. They also inform the [budget](./Budget.md): resources go to what the OKRs prioritize. A common mistake is writing key results as tasks ("launch new onboarding") rather than outcomes ("60% activation").

_Avoid:_ "KPI" as a synonym — a KPI is an ongoing measure; an OKR is a time-bound goal, often to move a KPI.

_Usage:_

"Is 'ship the new dashboard' a good key result?"

"It's a task, not a result. What should the dashboard change? Make that number the key result."
