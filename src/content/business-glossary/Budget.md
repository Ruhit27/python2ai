---
description: A plan for how much money will be earned and spent over a period, used to set limits and priorities.
---

A financial plan for a future period, usually a year, setting out expected [revenue](./Revenue.md) and how much each team or activity is allowed to spend. It turns strategy into numbers: whatever the company says is a priority should show up as money in the budget.

Budgets are built from a [forecast](./Forecast.md) of what's likely to happen, then used as a target and a limit. During the year, actual figures are compared to the budget; the difference is called a *variance*. Overspending on marketing by 20% or missing revenue by 10% prompts questions and adjustments.

A department's biggest line is often [headcount](./Headcount.md), since salaries usually outweigh other [costs](./Cost.md). Budgets also force trade-offs: every dollar given to one project isn't available for another, which is the idea of [opportunity cost](./Opportunity%20cost.md). The manager responsible for a budget is often the [P&L owner](./P%26L%20owner.md).

_Avoid:_ "forecast" — a forecast predicts what will happen; a budget sets what should happen.

_Usage:_

"Can we buy the new analytics tool?"

"It's not in this year's budget. We'd have to cut something else or wait for next year's planning."
