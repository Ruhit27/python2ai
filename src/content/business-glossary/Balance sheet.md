A snapshot of a business's finances on one specific date. It lists everything the company owns (its [assets](./Asset.md)), everything it owes (its [liabilities](./Liability.md)), and the difference, which belongs to the owners ([equity](./Equity.md)).

It always balances, because of the basic accounting equation: **assets = liabilities + equity**. If a coffee shop owns $80,000 of equipment, inventory, and cash, and owes $30,000 to the bank, the owners' equity is $50,000.

Where the [income statement](./Income%20statement.md) is like a video of a period, the balance sheet is a photo of a moment. Reading them together shows both how the business performed and where it stands — for example, whether profits are piling up as cash or disappearing into unpaid customer bills and [inventory](./Inventory.md).

_Avoid:_ "statement of financial position" is the formal international name for the same report.

_Usage:_

"Is the company actually solid?"

"Look at the balance sheet — they owe more than they own. Equity's negative."
