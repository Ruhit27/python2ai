---

Anything a business owns or controls that has value and can bring future benefit. For a coffee shop: the cash in the bank, the espresso machine, the beans in the storeroom ([inventory](./Inventory.md)), and money customers owe it.

Assets are usually split into **current assets** — cash or things that will become cash within a year, like inventory and unpaid customer bills — and **long-term assets**, like buildings, machines, and software, which are used over many years and lose value through [depreciation](./Depreciation.md). Some assets aren't physical at all: a brand, a patent, or a customer list can be listed as *intangible* assets.

Assets appear on the [balance sheet](./Balance%20sheet.md) opposite [liabilities](./Liability.md). Whatever the assets are worth after all liabilities are paid off is the owners' [equity](./Equity.md).

_Avoid:_ "resource" in financial talk — asset is the precise word.

_Usage:_

"Should we buy the van or lease it?"

"Buy it and it's an asset on our books that depreciates. Lease it and it's a monthly cost."
