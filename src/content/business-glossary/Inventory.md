The goods a business holds for sale or for making its products: raw materials, partly finished items, and finished products waiting to be sold. For a coffee shop: beans, milk, cups, and pastries.

Inventory is an [asset](./Asset.md) on the [balance sheet](./Balance%20sheet.md), but it's also cash that's tied up. Too much, and money sits in the storeroom, goods may spoil or go out of fashion, and storage costs pile up. Too little, and the business runs out and loses sales. Managing this balance is a central part of managing [working capital](./Working%20capital.md).

How much to hold depends on [lead time](./Lead%20time.md) (how long restocking takes), how steady demand is, and how reliable the [supply chain](./Supply%20chain.md) is. Some companies run *just-in-time*: holding almost no inventory and receiving materials exactly when needed. It's efficient but fragile when suppliers are late.

_Avoid:_ "stock" is the common name for inventory in the UK and elsewhere; fine, but can be confused with company shares.

_Usage:_

"Cash is tight but sales are up. Where did the money go?"

"Into inventory. We've got $80k of product sitting in the warehouse."
