Stands for **S**ervice **L**evel **A**greement: a written commitment from a provider to a customer about the level of service to expect, usually with measurable targets and consequences if they're missed.

Typical SLA terms: a website will be online 99.9% of the time (about 43 minutes of downtime a month); urgent support tickets get a reply within one hour; orders ship within two business days. If the provider misses the target, the customer usually gets money back as credits on their next bill.

SLAs are common in [B2B](./B2B%20and%20B2C.md) contracts and in [outsourcing](./Outsourcing.md), where they turn vague expectations into numbers both sides can check. Internally, the same targets often become [KPIs](./KPI.md) for the team delivering the service. Setting one needs care: promising a shorter [lead time](./Lead%20time.md) or higher uptime than you can reliably deliver creates a financial [liability](./Liability.md).

_Avoid:_ "guarantee" in the everyday sense — an SLA defines specific targets and remedies, not a general promise.

_Usage:_

"The client says we owe them a refund for last week's outage."

"Our SLA promises 99.9% uptime. We were down for three hours, so yes — they get the service credit."
