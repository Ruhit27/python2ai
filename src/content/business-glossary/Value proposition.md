A clear statement of why a particular customer should buy from you: what problem you solve, for whom, and why you do it better than the alternatives — including doing nothing.

A good value proposition is specific. "Great accounting software" says little. "Invoicing for freelance designers that gets you paid in half the time" names a customer, a problem, and a measurable benefit. It tells the customer why to care and tells the team what to focus on.

The value proposition sits at the heart of the [business model](./Business%20model.md) and underpins the [brand](./Brand.md). If customers don't find it convincing, the [funnel](./Funnel.md) leaks — low [conversion rates](./Conversion%20rate.md) and high [churn](./Churn.md) are often signs of a weak or unclear value proposition, not bad marketing. Reaching [product-market fit](./Product-market%20fit.md) means the value proposition works for a large enough market.

_Avoid:_ "features" — a value proposition is the benefit to the customer, not the list of things the product does.

_Usage:_

"Why aren't people signing up? The product has everything."

"But what's the one reason to switch from what they use now? Our value proposition isn't clear."
