---
description: Customer acquisition cost: how much you spend on sales and marketing to win one new customer.
---

Stands for **C**ustomer **A**cquisition **C**ost: the average amount spent on sales and marketing to win one new customer. Spend $50,000 on ads, sales salaries, and tools in a month, win 100 customers, and CAC is $500.

CAC on its own says little; it matters compared to how much a customer is worth. That's [LTV](./LTV.md). A $500 CAC is excellent if the average customer brings in $5,000 of profit over their lifetime, and ruinous if they bring in $200. A common rule of thumb is that LTV should be at least three times CAC.

CAC falls when the [funnel](./Funnel.md) works better — higher [conversion rates](./Conversion%20rate.md), better-quality [leads](./Lead.md) — or when more customers come through word of mouth and a strong [brand](./Brand.md). It tends to rise as a company grows, because the easiest customers are won first. In [B2B](./B2B%20and%20B2C.md) sales, CAC is often high because deals involve long sales processes.

_Avoid:_ "marketing cost" — CAC is marketing and sales cost divided by new customers.

_Usage:_

"Our ads cost only $2 per click!"

"Sure, but only one in 400 clicks becomes a customer. Our CAC is $800."
