description: Current assets minus current liabilities. The short-term cushion a business has to pay its bills.
---

The money a business has available for day-to-day running: current [assets](./Asset.md) (cash, [inventory](./Inventory.md), money customers owe) minus current [liabilities](./Liability.md) (bills and debts due within a year).

Positive working capital means the business should be able to pay what it owes soon. Negative working capital can mean trouble — or, for some business models, efficiency. A supermarket gets paid by shoppers instantly but pays its suppliers weeks later, so it runs on the suppliers' money.

Working capital explains many [cash flow](./Cash%20flow.md) surprises. A fast-growing manufacturer must buy more materials and wait longer for more customer payments, so growth ties up cash. That's why growing businesses often need funding — [debt](./Debt.md) or [equity](./Equity.md) — even when they're profitable.

_Avoid:_ "cash" — working capital includes inventory and money owed by customers that are not cash yet.

_Usage:_

"We just landed our biggest order ever, and the bank is worried?"

"We have to buy $300k of materials now and get paid in 90 days. That's a working capital gap."
