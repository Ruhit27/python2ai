---
description: What a company is estimated to be worth. For startups, set by what investors pay for a share of it.
---

An estimate of how much a whole company is worth. For a company on the stock market, it's roughly the share price times the number of shares. For a private company, it is agreed during a funding round or sale.

If an investor pays $2 million for 20% of a startup, that implies a **post-money valuation** of $10 million (the company's worth including the new money). Subtract the new money and the **pre-money valuation** is $8 million.

Valuations are based partly on numbers — [revenue](./Revenue.md), [profit](./Profit.md), [EBITDA](./EBITDA.md), growth — and partly on belief about the future: market size ([TAM](./TAM.md)), [competitive advantage](./Competitive%20advantage.md), and the team. That's why two similar companies can have very different valuations, and why a high valuation raises expectations the company then has to meet.

_Avoid:_ "price" for private companies — the valuation is an agreed number, not a price anyone can trade at.

_Usage:_

"They raised at a $50 million valuation, so they must be making money."

"Not necessarily. Valuation is a bet on the future; they might have hardly any revenue yet."
