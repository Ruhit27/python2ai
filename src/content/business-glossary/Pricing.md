Deciding how much to charge and in what form. Pricing covers not just the number but its structure: one-off or subscription, per user or per use, free tier or none, discounts for yearly plans.

There are three broad approaches. **Cost-plus** pricing adds a [margin](./Margin.md) on top of what it costs to make the product. **Competitor-based** pricing sets the price relative to alternatives. **Value-based** pricing charges according to how much the product is worth to the customer — software that saves a company $100,000 a year can charge far more than it costs to run.

Pricing is one of the strongest levers on [profit](./Profit.md): raising price by 5% with no drop in sales goes almost entirely to the bottom line. It also shapes who buys and how — a price that's too low can attract the wrong customers and increase [churn](./Churn.md), while a clear, well-set price can raise [conversion rates](./Conversion%20rate.md). It's part of the [business model](./Business%20model.md) and depends on the [brand](./Brand.md).

_Avoid:_ "price" — the price is the number; pricing is the whole approach behind it.

_Usage:_

"Should we add a cheaper plan to win more customers?"

"Maybe, but look at the pricing as a whole — a cheap plan might just move existing customers down."
