The actual money coming into and going out of a business over a period of time. Positive cash flow means more cash came in than went out; negative means the bank balance shrank.

Cash flow differs from [profit](./Profit.md) because of timing. A wholesaler might sell $50,000 of goods in March — profit on paper — but if the customer pays in 90 days while suppliers want paying now, the bank account goes down, not up. Many profitable businesses have failed because they ran out of cash while waiting to be paid. That's why managing [working capital](./Working%20capital.md) matters.

Cash flow is reported in the [cash flow statement](./Cash%20flow%20statement.md). For a young company that's losing money on purpose to grow, negative cash flow is described as its [burn rate](./Burn%20rate.md).

_Avoid:_ "profit" — a company can be profitable with negative cash flow, and the reverse.

_Usage:_

"We're profitable, so why can't we pay the suppliers this month?"

"Because our biggest customers pay in 60 days. Profit's fine; cash flow isn't."
