description: Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization. Profit with some costs left out, to compare how operations perform.
---

Stands for **E**arnings **B**efore **I**nterest, **T**axes, **D**epreciation and **A**mortization. It is [profit](./Profit.md) with four kinds of cost added back, to show how well the core business is running on its own.

The idea: interest depends on how the company is funded (lots of [debt](./Debt.md) or none), taxes depend on where it is based, and [depreciation](./Depreciation.md) and amortization are accounting entries that spread old purchases across years rather than cash spent today. Removing them makes it easier to compare two companies that do the same thing but are financed differently. EBITDA is used a lot in [valuations](./Valuation.md) — a company might be described as "worth 10× EBITDA".

The risk is that it hides real costs. Machines really do wear out and loans really do have to be paid. A company with strong EBITDA and heavy debt can still struggle, so EBITDA is best read alongside net profit and [cash flow](./Cash%20flow.md).

_Avoid:_ "profit" or "cash" — EBITDA is neither. Say which you mean.

_Usage:_

"They say they're making $2 million EBITDA."

"Sure, but they've got big loan repayments. Check the net profit before you call them healthy."
