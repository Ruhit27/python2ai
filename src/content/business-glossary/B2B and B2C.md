---
description: Business-to-business vs business-to-consumer: whether your customers are companies or individual people.
---

Two broad types of [business](./Business%20model.md), based on who the customer is. **B2B** (business-to-business) sells to companies: accounting software, industrial parts, office cleaning. **B2C** (business-to-consumer) sells to individual people: clothing, streaming, food delivery.

The difference changes almost everything. B2B deals are usually larger, slower, and involve several decision-makers ([stakeholders](./Stakeholder.md)) — a purchase might need approval from a manager, finance, and IT. Sales teams and relationships matter, [leads](./Lead.md) are fewer but worth more, and contracts often include [SLAs](./SLA.md). B2C sales are smaller and faster, decided by one person, and rely more on [brand](./Brand.md), advertising, and a smooth [funnel](./Funnel.md).

This shapes the numbers too: B2B companies often have higher [CAC](./CAC.md) and higher [LTV](./LTV.md); B2C companies need huge volumes. Some businesses do both, and some sit in between, such as B2B2C (selling through a business to its customers).

_Avoid:_ "enterprise" as a synonym for B2B — enterprise usually means selling to very large companies, one part of B2B.

_Usage:_

"Why does their sales cycle take six months?"

"It's B2B — every deal needs sign-off from IT security, finance, and a department head."
