---
description: Recording revenue when it's earned and costs when they're incurred, not when cash changes hands.
---

An accounting method that records [revenue](./Revenue.md) when it is earned and [costs](./Cost.md) when they are incurred, regardless of when the money actually moves. The opposite, *cash accounting*, records things only when cash comes in or goes out.

Example: a web agency finishes a $10,000 project in June and the client pays in August. Under accrual accounting, the $10,000 is June revenue. The agency's June [income statement](./Income%20statement.md) shows the profit, but its bank account won't see the money until August. Until then, the unpaid bill sits on the [balance sheet](./Balance%20sheet.md) as an [asset](./Asset.md) ("accounts receivable").

Accrual accounting gives a truer picture of how a business performed in a period, which is why most companies above a small size must use it. But it's also why [profit](./Profit.md) and [cash flow](./Cash%20flow.md) can tell different stories.

_Avoid:_ "when we got paid" as the definition of revenue timing — under accrual, getting paid is a separate event.

_Usage:_

"The client hasn't paid yet — does that project count for this quarter?"

"Yes, we use accrual. We did the work this quarter, so the revenue is this quarter."
