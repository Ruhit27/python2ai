description: Money or effort already spent that can't be recovered, and so shouldn't affect what you decide next.
---

Money, time, or effort that has already been spent and cannot be recovered, whatever you decide next. Because it's gone either way, it shouldn't influence future decisions — only future [costs](./Cost.md) and benefits should.

The **sunk cost fallacy** is the common mistake of continuing something because of what's already been put into it. "We've spent $400,000 on this project, we can't stop now" is sunk cost thinking. The right question is: from here, is the remaining cost worth the remaining benefit? If finishing costs another $200,000 for $100,000 of value, it should stop, regardless of the $400,000 already gone.

Sunk costs are the backward-looking mirror of [opportunity cost](./Opportunity%20cost.md): one is what's gone, the other what you give up next. Good [ROI](./ROI.md) calculations for a decision include only costs still to come. Teams defending a [budget](./Budget.md) line or a long project are especially prone to the fallacy, because stopping feels like admitting waste.

_Avoid:_ "wasted money" — a sunk cost is not necessarily wasted; it is just no longer relevant to the next decision.

_Usage:_

"We've invested two years in this product — we have to keep going."

"Those two years are a sunk cost. The question is whether the next six months are worth it."
