How a business creates value and turns it into [revenue](./Revenue.md) and eventually [profit](./Profit.md). It answers: who is the customer, what do they pay for, how much, how often, and what does it cost to deliver?

Common models include selling a product once (a bakery), subscriptions (a streaming service or SaaS tool, paid monthly), marketplaces taking a cut of each sale (a food delivery app), advertising (a free app paid for by advertisers), and licensing (charging others to use your technology or brand). The same product can be sold under different models — software can be a one-off purchase or a subscription — and the choice shapes everything from [pricing](./Pricing.md) to [cash flow](./Cash%20flow.md).

A business model is only as good as its **unit economics**: whether each customer brings in more than they cost to win and serve. That is what [CAC](./CAC.md) and [LTV](./LTV.md) measure. The model also has to deliver a real [value proposition](./Value%20proposition.md).

_Avoid:_ "strategy" — strategy is how you plan to win; the business model is how you make money.

_Usage:_

"We have a million free users. What's the business model?"

"That's the problem — we haven't worked out who pays yet."
