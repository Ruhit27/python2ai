---
description: A share of a company's profit paid out in cash to its shareholders.
---

A payment a company makes to its [shareholders](./Shareholder.md) out of its [profits](./Profit.md), usually in cash and usually per share. If a company pays a $0.50 dividend and you own 1,000 shares, you receive $500.

A profitable company can either keep its profits to reinvest — hiring, building, expanding — or return some to its owners as dividends. Mature, stable businesses with steady [cash flow](./Cash%20flow.md), like utilities or large consumer brands, often pay regular dividends. Fast-growing companies, especially those backed by [venture capital](./Venture%20capital.md), usually pay none: investors expect the money to go into growth, which should raise the company's [valuation](./Valuation.md).

Dividends paid usually appear in the financing section of the [cash flow statement](./Cash%20flow%20statement.md), because they are money returned to owners, though some accounting rules also allow them under operating.

_Avoid:_ "interest" — interest is paid to lenders on debt; dividends are paid to owners, and only if the company chooses to.

_Usage:_

"Why doesn't the company pay dividends if it's profitable?"

"It's reinvesting every dollar into expanding to new cities. Shareholders are betting on growth instead."
