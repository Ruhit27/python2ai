description: What is left when all costs are taken away from revenue. The bottom line. Negative profit is a loss.
---

What remains when all [costs](./Cost.md) are subtracted from [revenue](./Revenue.md). If a coffee shop earns $4,000 in a month and spends $3,400, its profit is $600. If it spends $4,500, it has made a $500 *loss*.

There are several profits, depending on which costs you take away: **gross profit** subtracts only the cost of making the product, **operating profit** also subtracts the costs of running the business, and **net profit** (or *net income*) subtracts everything, including interest on [debt](./Debt.md) and taxes. Net profit is the "bottom line" of the [income statement](./Income%20statement.md). [EBITDA](./EBITDA.md) is a related measure that leaves some costs out.

Profit is not the same as cash. A business can be profitable on paper and still run out of money if customers pay late — which is why [cash flow](./Cash%20flow.md) is watched separately.

_Avoid:_ "earnings" and "net income" are synonyms; "income" alone is ambiguous, so say which profit you mean.

_Usage:_

"Are we profitable?"

"Gross profit, yes. Net, no — once rent and salaries come off we're still slightly under."
