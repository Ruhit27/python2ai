---
description: When a company issues new shares, each existing owner's percentage goes down, even though their number of shares stays the same.
---

The drop in an existing owner's percentage of a company when new shares are created. The number of shares they hold stays the same, but it becomes a smaller slice of a bigger total.

Example: a founder owns 800,000 of 1,000,000 shares — 80%. The company creates 250,000 new shares to sell to a [venture capital](./Venture%20capital.md) fund. The founder still has 800,000 shares, but now out of 1,250,000: 64%. They've been diluted.

Dilution isn't necessarily a loss. If the new money raises the company's [valuation](./Valuation.md), a smaller percentage of a larger company can be worth more. It's the normal price of raising [equity](./Equity.md) funding, and founders plan how much they'll give up in each round. Raising [debt](./Debt.md) instead avoids dilution, but has to be repaid.

_Avoid:_ "losing shares" — diluted owners keep all their shares; their percentage shrinks.

_Usage:_

"Another funding round? My 1% is going to shrink."

"It'll dilute to about 0.8%, but if the round doubles the valuation, your stake is worth more than before."
