description: The point where revenue exactly covers costs: no profit, no loss. Below it you lose money, above it you make it.
---

The point where [revenue](./Revenue.md) exactly equals total [costs](./Cost.md), so the business makes neither a [profit](./Profit.md) nor a loss. Every sale after that point adds profit.

The break-even point comes from fixed and variable costs. If a coffee shop has $3,000 of fixed costs a month and each $4 coffee costs $1 to make, each coffee contributes $3 toward the fixed costs. It needs 1,000 coffees a month to break even. The 1,001st coffee is the first one that makes money.

Break-even can also be measured in time: "we'll break even in month 18" means the business expects to stop losing money then. Founders and investors use it to judge how long the money will need to last — which links it to [burn rate](./Burn%20rate.md).

_Avoid:_ "profitable" — at break-even you have exactly zero profit.

_Usage:_

"How many subscriptions do we need to break even?"

"About 400 at the current price. We're at 310."
