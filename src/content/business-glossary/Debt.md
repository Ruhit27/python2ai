description: Money borrowed that must be paid back, usually with interest. Doesn't give the lender any ownership.
---

Money a business borrows and must pay back, usually with interest on top. Bank loans, bonds, credit lines, and credit cards are all debt. Debt is one kind of [liability](./Liability.md).

Debt and [equity](./Equity.md) are the two main ways to fund a business. Debt keeps ownership intact — the bank gets its money back plus interest, but no share of the company, so there's no [dilution](./Dilution.md). The catch is that repayments are due whatever happens. A business with falling [revenue](./Revenue.md) still has to pay, which is why too much debt can sink an otherwise sound company.

Businesses with steady, predictable [cash flow](./Cash%20flow.md) — a well-established shop or factory — can often carry debt safely. Young, risky startups usually can't get much debt and rely on [venture capital](./Venture%20capital.md) instead.

_Avoid:_ "liability" as a full synonym — all debt is a liability, but not all liabilities are borrowed money.

_Usage:_

"Should we raise money from investors or take a loan?"

"A loan means no dilution, but we'd need to repay $8k a month from day one. Our cash flow can't cover that yet."
