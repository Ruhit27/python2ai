A prediction of future numbers — [revenue](./Revenue.md), [costs](./Cost.md), [cash](./Cash%20flow.md), sales, [headcount](./Headcount.md) — based on past data, current trends, and stated assumptions. "We forecast $1.2 million revenue next quarter, assuming 5% monthly growth and churn stays at 3%."

Unlike a [budget](./Budget.md), which is set once and used as a target, a forecast is updated regularly as new information arrives. Many companies re-forecast monthly or quarterly: the budget says what they planned; the forecast says where they're actually heading.

Forecasts are only as good as their assumptions, so good ones state those assumptions clearly and often show a range — a cautious, expected, and optimistic case. Companies that sell shares to the public after an [IPO](./IPO.md) are judged heavily against the forecasts they publish, and a missed forecast can move the share price sharply.

_Avoid:_ "budget" — a budget is a plan and a limit; a forecast is a best guess that changes.

_Usage:_

"We budgeted $5 million revenue. Are we on track?"

"The latest forecast says $4.4 million — two big deals slipped to next year."
