---
description: All the money a business earns from selling what it sells, before anything is taken away. The top line.
---

All the money a business brings in from selling its products or services, before any [costs](./Cost.md) are subtracted. A coffee shop that sells 1,000 coffees at $4 each has $4,000 of revenue that month, however much it spent on beans, rent, and staff.

Revenue is called the "top line" because it sits on the first line of the [income statement](./Income%20statement.md); [profit](./Profit.md) is the "bottom line" because it's what is left at the end. A business can grow revenue fast and still lose money, if its costs grow faster.

Revenue is counted when it is *earned*, not when the cash arrives. A software company that sells a $1,200 yearly plan in January usually counts $100 of revenue each month, because it delivers the service month by month. That timing rule is [accrual](./Accrual.md) accounting, and it's why revenue and [cash flow](./Cash%20flow.md) often differ.

_Avoid:_ "sales" or "turnover" are common synonyms, and fine; avoid "income" on its own, which is ambiguous and often means profit.

_Usage:_

"We doubled revenue this year!"

"Great — but did [profit](./Profit.md) double too, or did we just spend twice as much to get it?"
