description: The value of the best alternative you give up when you choose one option over another.
---

The value of the best alternative you give up when you make a choice. Every decision to spend time, money, or people on one thing is a decision not to spend it on something else.

If a small bakery uses its only free weekend to cater a wedding for $2,000 profit, the opportunity cost is whatever else that weekend could have earned — say, a food market stall worth $3,000. On paper the wedding looks profitable; against the alternative, it was a $1,000 mistake.

Opportunity cost doesn't appear in any financial statement, which is why it's easy to overlook. It's central to good decisions about [budgets](./Budget.md), [headcount](./Headcount.md), and priorities — and it's the missing piece in many [ROI](./ROI.md) calculations, which compare an option to doing nothing rather than to the next-best option. It's the opposite in spirit to a [sunk cost](./Sunk%20cost.md): opportunity cost looks forward at what you could still do; sunk cost looks back at what's already spent.

_Avoid:_ "cost" in the accounting sense — no money changes hands; it is value you forgo.

_Usage:_

"The client wants a custom feature for $20k. Easy money, right?"

"It would take the whole team a month. The opportunity cost is the feature every customer's been asking for."
