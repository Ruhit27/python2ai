---
description: When a product satisfies a real market so well that customers keep buying and telling others, with little pushing.
---

The stage where a product clearly meets a strong need in a large enough market. Customers buy, stay, and recommend it to others without the company having to push hard. The term is especially common among startups, where finding it is the first major goal.

Signs of product-market fit include low [churn](./Churn.md), rising usage, customers who would be very disappointed if the product disappeared, word-of-mouth growth, and sales that get easier over time rather than harder. Before fit, growth is a struggle: the [funnel](./Funnel.md) leaks, [CAC](./CAC.md) is high, and changes to the product matter more than marketing.

Product-market fit means the [value proposition](./Value%20proposition.md) works for enough people. It isn't permanent — markets move and competitors appear — and it isn't the same as a sound [business model](./Business%20model.md); a product people love can still fail to make money. [VC](./Venture%20capital.md) investors often wait for signs of it before investing heavily.

_Avoid:_ "launch" or "traction" — you can launch and have some traction long before real product-market fit.

_Usage:_

"Should we double the marketing budget?"

"Not yet. Half our users leave in the first month. We don't have product-market fit — more marketing will just fill a leaking bucket."
