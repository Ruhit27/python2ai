---
description: Investment funds that buy equity in young, risky, fast-growing companies, hoping a few become huge.
---

Money invested in young companies that could grow very large, in exchange for [equity](./Equity.md). The investors are venture capital (VC) firms, which raise funds from pension funds, universities, and wealthy people, then invest across many startups.

VCs expect most of their investments to fail or return little. They make money from the few that become enormous — through an [IPO](./IPO.md) or by being bought by a larger company. That's why they look for huge markets ([TAM](./TAM.md)), fast growth, and ideally [product-market fit](./Product-market%20fit.md), rather than steady modest [profits](./Profit.md).

Funding usually comes in rounds: seed, Series A, Series B and so on, each at a higher [valuation](./Valuation.md) if things go well, and each causing [dilution](./Dilution.md). VC money lets a company run at a loss for years — high [burn rate](./Burn%20rate.md) — to grow first and profit later. That suits some businesses and pushes others to grow faster than is healthy.

_Avoid:_ "investor" in general — VCs are one specific kind, with specific expectations about growth.

_Usage:_

"Should our bakery raise VC?"

"Probably not. VCs need a chance at 100× returns. A profitable bakery is a great business, but it's not a VC business."
