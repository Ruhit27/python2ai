---
description: The stages a person moves through from first hearing about you to buying, with fewer people at each step.
---

A model of the steps a potential customer goes through on the way to buying, drawn as a funnel because fewer people make it through each stage. A typical online funnel:

| Stage | People |
| --- | --- |
| See an ad or article | 10,000 |
| Visit the website | 1,000 |
| Sign up for a free trial | 100 |
| Become paying customers | 20 |

Each step has a [conversion rate](./Conversion%20rate.md) — here, 10%, 10%, and 20%. Looking at the funnel stage by stage shows where people drop off, so a team knows what to fix: more traffic, a clearer sign-up page, or a better trial.

The top of the funnel is about attention and [leads](./Lead.md); the bottom is about closing sales. Improving any stage lowers [CAC](./CAC.md). Many companies extend the funnel past purchase to include retention and referrals, because keeping customers (low [churn](./Churn.md)) is often cheaper than finding new ones.

_Avoid:_ "pipeline" — in sales, the pipeline is the list of specific deals in progress; the funnel is the overall shape of stages and drop-off.

_Usage:_

"Sign-ups are fine but revenue is flat."

"Then the problem is lower in the funnel — only 5% of trials convert to paid."
