description: Every step and company involved in getting a product from raw materials to the customer.
---

The whole network of steps, companies, and movements needed to get a product from raw materials to the final customer. For a coffee shop's latte: the farm that grows the beans, the exporter, the shipping company, the roaster, the dairy, the cup maker, the distributor, and finally the shop.

Every link adds [cost](./Cost.md) and time ([lead time](./Lead%20time.md)), and every link can fail. A delay at one port can leave shelves empty worldwide, as many businesses learned during the 2020–2022 disruptions. Companies manage supply chains by balancing cost, speed, and resilience — holding more [inventory](./Inventory.md), using more than one supplier, or moving production closer to customers.

Supply chain decisions tie into [outsourcing](./Outsourcing.md) (which steps to do yourself and which to buy in), [economies of scale](./Economies%20of%20scale.md) (bigger buyers get better terms), and [working capital](./Working%20capital.md) (inventory sitting in warehouses is cash that isn't in the bank).

_Avoid:_ "logistics" as a synonym — logistics is the moving and storing part of the supply chain, not the whole thing.

_Usage:_

"Why are we out of stock when demand is normal?"

"Supply chain — our supplier's factory closed for two weeks and they're our only source."
