---
description: Something that lets a business beat its rivals in a way that's hard to copy. Also called a moat.
---

Something that lets a business do better than its competitors — win more customers, charge more, or spend less — in a way that others can't easily copy. A lasting competitive advantage is often called a **moat**, like the water around a castle keeping attackers out.

Common sources: lower [costs](./Cost.md) (often from [economies of scale](./Economies%20of%20scale.md)), a strong [brand](./Brand.md) people trust, network effects (a marketplace is more useful the more people use it), high switching costs (moving all your company's data to a new tool is painful), patents, or exclusive access to something scarce.

A good product on its own is often not a competitive advantage, because a well-funded rival can copy features. Investors value companies with strong moats more highly, because their [profits](./Profit.md) and [market share](./Market%20share.md) are more likely to last — which lifts the [valuation](./Valuation.md).

_Avoid:_ "USP" (unique selling point) is related but narrower: a feature to advertise, not necessarily something rivals cannot copy.

_Usage:_

"A big competitor just launched the same feature."

"That's why the feature was never our moat. Our advantage is the integrations — customers can't switch without redoing their whole setup."
