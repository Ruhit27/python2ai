---

How quickly a company is losing cash, usually measured per month. If a startup has $120,000 coming in and $200,000 going out each month, its burn rate is $80,000 a month.

Burn rate matters most for young companies that are losing money on purpose, using [investors'](./Venture%20capital.md) money to grow before they reach [break-even](./Break-even.md). Divide the cash in the bank by the monthly burn and you get the **runway**: how many months until the money runs out. $960,000 in the bank at $80,000 a month is a 12-month runway.

Burn is simply negative [cash flow](./Cash%20flow.md) described from a startup's point of view. A high burn isn't automatically bad — it can mean fast investment in growth — but it sets a hard deadline to either become profitable or raise more money, which usually means [dilution](./Dilution.md).

_Avoid:_ "spend" — burn is spend minus income. A company can spend a lot and burn little.

_Usage:_

"Can we afford two more hires?"

"They'd push burn to $95k a month and cut our runway from 14 months to 11. Worth it only if they speed up sales."
