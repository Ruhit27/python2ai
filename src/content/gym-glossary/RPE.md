Stands for **R**ate of **P**erceived **E**xertion: a scale for how hard a [set](./Set.md) felt. In lifting, it usually runs from 1 to 10, where **10** means you couldn't have done another [rep](./Rep.md) ([failure](./Training%20to%20failure.md)), **9** means you had one more in you, **8** means two more, and so on.

RPE lets a [program](./Program.md) adjust to how you are on the day. Instead of "5 reps at 100 kg (220 lb)", it might say "5 reps at RPE 8": pick a weight where you'd have about two reps left. On a good day that might be 102.5 kg (225 lb); after bad [sleep](./Sleep.md), 95 kg (210 lb). It's the reverse view of [RIR](./RIR.md) — RPE 8 is the same as 2 RIR.

Beginners often misjudge RPE, usually thinking sets are harder than they are. It gets more accurate with practice, and occasionally taking a set to true failure (safely) helps calibrate it. In cardio, a similar RPE scale describes overall effort instead of reps left.

_Avoid:_ "it felt like a 10" when you could clearly have done more — RPE 10 means no more reps were possible.

_Usage:_

"The program says RPE 7 — how heavy is that?"

"Heavy enough that you could do about three more reps, but no more."
