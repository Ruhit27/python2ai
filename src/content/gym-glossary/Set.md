A group of [reps](./Rep.md) done back to back, followed by a [rest](./Rest%20period.md). "3 × 10" (read "three sets of ten") means: do 10 reps, rest, do 10 more, rest, then a final 10.

Sets are the main way training is counted. The number of hard sets you do for a muscle each week is the simplest measure of training [volume](./Volume.md), and volume is one of the biggest drivers of muscle growth. Beginners make progress with surprisingly few sets; more experienced lifters usually need more.

There are several kinds of sets. **Warm-up sets** use lighter weights to prepare for the heavy ones (see [warm-up](./Warm-up.md)). **Working sets** are the ones that count. Programs may also use *drop sets* (lowering the weight and continuing without rest) or *supersets* (two exercises back to back) to fit more work into less time.

_Avoid:_ "round" or "circuit" when you mean a set — those usually mean going through several different exercises in a row.

_Usage:_

"I did bench today — five sets!"

"Were they all working sets, or were some warm-ups? Only the hard ones really count toward your volume."
