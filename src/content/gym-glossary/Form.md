The way an exercise is performed: how the body is positioned, how the weight moves, and how controlled the movement is. "Good form" means doing the exercise in a way that trains the intended muscles through a full [range of motion](./Range%20of%20motion.md) while keeping stress on the joints reasonable. It's also called *technique*.

Good form looks slightly different for everyone, because bodies differ in limb length and mobility — there's no single perfect squat. But the basics are shared: a stable position, a controlled [tempo](./Tempo.md), and no big jerks or twists to force the weight up. When form breaks down near the end of a set, it's usually a sign you're at or near [failure](./Training%20to%20failure.md).

Beginners progress fastest by adding weight only as fast as their form allows. Letting form slide to lift more than you can control is known in gym slang as *ego lifting*. Filming a set from the side, or asking an experienced lifter or coach, is the easiest way to check it. Poor form is often blamed for [injuries](./Injury%20and%20soreness.md), but sudden big jumps in load or [volume](./Volume.md) are just as common a cause.

_Avoid:_ "perfect form" as one fixed shape — good form varies with your body; the principles are what stay the same.

_Usage:_

"My lower back hurts after deadlifts."

"Film your next set. If your back rounds as the bar leaves the floor, drop the weight and work on form."
