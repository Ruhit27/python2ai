description: How much force your muscles can produce — in the gym, usually how much weight you can lift. Related to, but not the same as, size.
---

The ability to produce force — in practice, how much weight you can move. It's usually measured with a [one-rep max](./One-rep%20max.md) or a heavy set of a few [reps](./Rep.md).

Strength and muscle size ([hypertrophy](./Muscle%20hypertrophy.md)) are linked — bigger muscles can produce more force — but they're trained a little differently. Strength depends heavily on the nervous system: how well it recruits muscle fibers and coordinates the movement. That's why beginners get much stronger in the first months before much muscle is added, and why practicing heavy, low-rep sets of a specific lift makes you stronger at *that* lift.

Heavy training — high [intensity](./Intensity.md), 1–6 reps — builds maximal strength best; moderate reps with more [volume](./Volume.md) builds size best. Most people training for general fitness get plenty of both from a balanced [program](./Program.md) with [progressive overload](./Progressive%20overload.md).

_Avoid:_ "strong" and "big" as the same — two people with similar muscles can lift very different weights.

_Usage:_

"He's smaller than me but lifts more. How?"

"Strength is a skill as well as muscle. He trains heavy, low-rep sets of the same lifts."
