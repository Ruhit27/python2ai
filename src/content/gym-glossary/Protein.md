description: A nutrient made of amino acids that muscles use to repair and grow after training. From the gym side: the raw material for gains.
---

One of the three main nutrients in food, made of building blocks called amino acids. For lifters, protein is the raw material for [recovery](./Recovery.md): after training, muscles use it to repair and build new muscle tissue — [hypertrophy](./Muscle%20hypertrophy.md).

Training creates the signal to grow; protein supplies the materials. Research on people who lift finds that eating more protein than the basic health guideline improves muscle gain, up to roughly 1.6 grams per kilogram of bodyweight per day (about 0.7 g per lb), with little extra benefit beyond about 2.2 g/kg (1 g/lb) for most people. Spreading it across several meals seems to work slightly better than one big dose.

Protein matters in every phase — during a [bulk](./Bulking.md) to build muscle, and especially during a [cut](./Cutting.md), when it helps keep muscle while fat is lost. The nutrition side — food sources, protein quality, and how it's digested — is covered in the Nutrition Glossary.

_Avoid:_ "the anabolic window" — you don't have to drink a shake within 30 minutes of training; total daily protein matters far more.

_Usage:_

"Do I need a shake right after the gym?"

"Only if it's the easiest way to hit your daily protein. The timing hardly matters."
