---
description: How well you can move a joint through its full range under control. Different from flexibility, which is passive range.
---

The ability to actively move a joint through its full [range of motion](./Range%20of%20motion.md) with control. For example, being able to [squat](./Squat.md) deep with a straight back and heels on the floor requires good ankle, hip, and upper-back mobility.

Mobility is related to flexibility but not the same. **Flexibility** is how far a muscle can be stretched passively — someone else pushing your leg up. **Mobility** is how much of that range you can use yourself, under load. You can be flexible but lack the strength and control to use that range.

The best way to improve mobility for lifting is often simply training through a full range of motion with good [form](./Form.md): lifting itself improves mobility. Targeted drills and [stretching](./Stretching.md) help for specific limits, and a dynamic [warm-up](./Warm-up.md) prepares joints before a session.

_Avoid:_ "mobility" and "flexibility" as the same — flexibility is passive range; mobility is range you control.

_Usage:_

"My heels come up when I squat."

"That's usually ankle mobility. Try a few ankle drills in your warm-up, or elevate your heels a little."
