---
description: How much training you do, usually counted as hard sets per muscle per week. A key driver of muscle growth.
---

The amount of training you do. It can be counted as [sets](./Set.md) × [reps](./Rep.md) × weight (total *tonnage*), but the most practical measure is simpler: **the number of hard sets per muscle per week**.

Volume is one of the strongest drivers of [muscle growth](./Muscle%20hypertrophy.md). Research shows that doing more sets tends to produce more growth, up to a point. Many lifters grow well on roughly 10–20 hard sets per [muscle group](./Muscle%20group.md) per week, though beginners can grow on fewer. More isn't endlessly better: past what you can recover from, extra sets just add fatigue.

Volume is the opposite dial to [intensity](./Intensity.md): very heavy training usually means fewer reps, while lighter training allows more. How you spread volume across the week is [training frequency](./Training%20frequency.md). Easy sets done far from failure add fatigue without much growth; gym slang calls them *junk volume*. When fatigue builds up, a [deload](./Deload.md) cuts volume temporarily.

_Avoid:_ "more sets is always better" — past what you can recover from, extra sets add fatigue, not growth.

_Usage:_

"My chest isn't growing."

"You're doing four sets a week for it. Most lifters need more than that — build up gradually."
