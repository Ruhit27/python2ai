Stands for **H**igh-**I**ntensity **I**nterval **T**raining: [cardio](./Cardio.md) made of short bursts of very hard effort separated by rest or easy recovery. For example, 30 seconds of all-out cycling followed by 90 seconds of easy pedaling, repeated 6–10 times.

HIIT is time-efficient: a 15–20 minute session can improve [VO2 max](./VO2%20max.md) and fitness about as much as a much longer steady session. The downside is that it's very tiring. Done too often, it adds a lot of fatigue that can interfere with lifting and [recovery](./Recovery.md).

A common balance is one or two HIIT sessions a week alongside lower-intensity work like [LISS](./LISS.md) or [Zone 2](./Zone%202.md). Claims that HIIT burns fat for hours afterwards are overstated — the extra "afterburn" is real but small.

_Avoid:_ "any hard circuit is HIIT" — true HIIT means very hard intervals with deliberate rest, not just a tiring class.

_Usage:_

"I only have 20 minutes for cardio."

"Do HIIT on the bike: 30 seconds hard, 90 easy, eight rounds."
