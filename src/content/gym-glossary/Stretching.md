---
description: Lengthening a muscle to improve flexibility. Static stretching holds a position; dynamic stretching moves through it.
---

Deliberately lengthening a muscle to improve flexibility. There are two main kinds. **Static** stretching means holding a stretch still, often for 20–60 seconds. **Dynamic** stretching means moving through a range repeatedly and under control, like leg swings or arm circles.

Research has changed advice on stretching. Long static stretches right before lifting can briefly reduce [strength](./Strength.md) and power, so a dynamic [warm-up](./Warm-up.md) is usually better before training. Stretching also doesn't reliably prevent [DOMS](./DOMS.md) or most [injuries](./Injury%20and%20soreness.md).

Stretching does improve flexibility over time, which can help with [mobility](./Mobility.md) and with getting into positions like a deep [squat](./Squat.md). Doing it after training or as a separate session is a good fit. Lifting with a full [range of motion](./Range%20of%20motion.md) also improves flexibility, often as much as stretching does.

_Avoid:_ "stretch before lifting to prevent injury" — a dynamic warm-up does that job better than long static holds.

_Usage:_

"Should I stretch before squats?"

"Do a proper warm-up with lighter squats instead. Save long stretches for after."
