A single muscle cell — long, thin, and able to contract. A muscle is made of thousands of fibers bundled together. When you lift, your nervous system switches on (recruits) fibers to produce force; heavier or harder efforts recruit more of them.

Fibers come in two broad types. **Slow-twitch** (type I) fibers are resistant to fatigue and suited to endurance, like [cardio](./Cardio.md). **Fast-twitch** (type II) fibers produce more force and power but tire quickly; they grow more readily with lifting. Everyone has a mix, and the proportions vary between people and muscles.

[Hypertrophy](./Muscle%20hypertrophy.md) is mostly fibers getting thicker, not new ones being added. Taking sets close to [failure](./Training%20to%20failure.md) matters because the fast-twitch fibers — the ones with the most growth potential — are only fully recruited as effort gets high, whether the weight is heavy or the set is long.

_Avoid:_ "muscle turns into fat" — muscle fibers and fat cells are completely different tissue; one can't become the other.

_Usage:_

"Why do light sets need to go close to failure?"

"Your biggest muscle fibers only switch on when the set gets really hard."
