---
description: Pressing a weight from the shoulders to straight overhead while standing or seated. Also called the shoulder press.
---

An exercise where you press a barbell or dumbbells from shoulder height to straight overhead. Done standing, it's often called the *military press* or *OHP*; seated, a *shoulder press*. It works the shoulders (mainly the front), the triceps, and the upper chest, while the core works hard to keep you stable.

It's a key [compound](./Compound%20exercise.md) pushing movement, often paired with the [bench press](./Bench%20press.md) in [programs](./Program.md). Progress on it is slower than on most lifts: the shoulders are relatively small muscles, so adding even 1–2 kg (2–5 lb) can be a big jump. Smaller weight plates help here.

Good [form](./Form.md) keeps the ribs down and glutes squeezed, so the lower back doesn't arch hard, and moves the head slightly back to let the bar travel straight up.

_Avoid:_ "shoulder day needs ten exercises" — the overhead press alone covers much of the shoulders' front and middle.

_Usage:_

"I can bench 80 kg (175 lb) but only press 45 kg (100 lb). Is something wrong?"

"No, that's normal. The overhead press is always much lighter than the bench."
