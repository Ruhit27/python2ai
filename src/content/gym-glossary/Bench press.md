description: Lying on a bench and pressing a barbell or dumbbells up from the chest. The main upper-body pushing lift.
---

An exercise where you lie on your back on a bench, lower a barbell (or dumbbells) to your chest, and press it back up. It mainly works the chest, the front of the shoulders, and the triceps. It's one of the three powerlifting lifts.

The bench press is the most common measure of upper-body [strength](./Strength.md) in gyms ("how much do you bench?"). Variations change which muscles work hardest: an *incline* bench (bench tilted up) shifts work toward the upper chest and shoulders; a *close-grip* bench works the triceps more; *dumbbell* presses allow more [range of motion](./Range%20of%20motion.md).

Good [form](./Form.md) keeps the shoulder blades pulled back and the feet planted. Because failing a rep can trap you under the bar, heavy [sets](./Set.md) should be done with a spotter or safety bars, especially when [training close to failure](./Training%20to%20failure.md).

_Avoid:_ "chest day" as only bench press — the chest can be trained with many exercises, and the bench also works shoulders and triceps.

_Usage:_

"Can I bench alone?"

"Yes, but set the safety bars just below chest height, and don't go to failure without them."
