description: A stretch of weeks where progress stalls despite training — the lifts, or the body, stop changing.
---

A period when progress stops — the weights you lift, your body shape, or your [muscle growth](./Muscle%20hypertrophy.md) stay the same for weeks despite consistent training. Everyone hits them eventually; beginners' fast progress always slows down.

Common causes: not enough [progressive overload](./Progressive%20overload.md) (doing the same thing each week), not enough [recovery](./Recovery.md) (poor [sleep](./Sleep.md), too much fatigue), not enough food — especially [protein](./Protein.md) or total calories — or a [program](./Program.md) that no longer fits your level.

Before changing everything, check the basics: sleep, food, and whether you're actually adding weight or reps. Common fixes include a [deload](./Deload.md), changing the rep ranges or exercises for a new block ([periodization](./Periodization.md)), or moving from a beginner program to an intermediate one. Some plateaus are simply the slower pace of experienced lifting mistaken for a stop.

_Avoid:_ "I've hit my genetic limit" after a few weeks without progress — true limits take years to reach.

_Usage:_

"My bench has been stuck at 70 kg (155 lb) for two months."

"That's a plateau. Are you eating enough? If so, let's deload and switch to a new rep scheme."
