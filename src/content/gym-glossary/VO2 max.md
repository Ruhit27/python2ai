---
description: The maximum amount of oxygen your body can use during hard exercise. A key measure of aerobic fitness.
---

The highest rate at which your body can take in and use oxygen during all-out exercise, usually expressed as millilitres of oxygen per kilogram of bodyweight per minute. It's one of the best single measures of cardiovascular fitness.

A higher VO2 max means your heart, lungs, and muscles can deliver and use more oxygen, so you can sustain harder efforts. It is strongly linked with long-term health: research consistently finds that people with higher VO2 max tend to live longer, which is why it has become popular beyond endurance sport.

VO2 max is partly genetic, but training raises it — especially [HIIT](./HIIT.md) and hard intervals, supported by plenty of easier [Zone 2](./Zone%202.md) [cardio](./Cardio.md). It's measured precisely in a lab with a mask on a treadmill or bike; fitness watches estimate it from [heart rate](./Heart%20rate%20zones.md) data, less accurately.

_Avoid:_ "my watch says my VO2 max is X" as exact — wearables give a rough estimate, useful mainly for trends.

_Usage:_

"My watch says my VO2 max went up."

"Nice — probably those interval sessions. Watch the trend rather than the exact number."
