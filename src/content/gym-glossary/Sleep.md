The single most important part of [recovery](./Recovery.md). During sleep, the body releases hormones involved in repair, consolidates the skills practiced in training, and restores energy. Most adults need around 7–9 hours a night.

Poor sleep hurts training in measurable ways: [strength](./Strength.md) and endurance drop, effort feels harder (sets at the same weight feel like a higher [RPE](./RPE.md)), and the risk of getting hurt rises. One well-known study found that people who slept less during a [cut](./Cutting.md) lost more muscle and less fat for the same calorie deficit.

The basics help most: a consistent schedule, a dark and cool room, and limiting caffeine late in the day and screens before bed. No supplement or recovery gadget makes up for regularly short sleep.

_Avoid:_ "I'll sleep when I'm dead" — cutting sleep undermines the training you're giving up sleep for.

_Usage:_

"My lifts are down this week."

"How's your sleep? Five hours a night will do that."
