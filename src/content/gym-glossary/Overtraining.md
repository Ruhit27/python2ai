description: Training far beyond what you can recover from for a long time, leading to a long drop in performance. True overtraining is rare.

A state where training stress has far exceeded [recovery](./Recovery.md) for a long period, causing performance to fall for weeks or months, along with fatigue, poor [sleep](./Sleep.md), mood changes, and frequent illness. Sports scientists call the full condition *overtraining syndrome*.

True overtraining is rare in recreational lifters — it mostly happens in endurance athletes doing very high volumes. What most gym-goers experience is **overreaching**: a short-term build-up of fatigue where performance dips for days to a couple of weeks. That's fixed with a [deload](./Deload.md) or some extra rest.

Signs to watch: lifts going backwards over several sessions, aches that don't go away, and training starting to feel like a chore. More often than too much training, the real problem is too little sleep, too little food (especially during a [cut](./Cutting.md)), or too much life stress. A sensible [program](./Program.md) with [periodization](./Periodization.md) keeps overreaching planned and short.

_Avoid:_ "I'm overtrained" after a tough week — that's usually short-term fatigue, and a few easy days fix it.

_Usage:_

"I think I'm overtrained — my bench dropped this week."

"More likely you're just tired. Deload for a week and see if it bounces back."
