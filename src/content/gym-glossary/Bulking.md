A phase of deliberately eating more calories than you burn, to support faster [muscle growth](./Muscle%20hypertrophy.md). Some of the extra weight gained will be fat, so bulking is usually followed by a [cut](./Cutting.md).

From the training side, the point of a bulk is that the extra energy and [protein](./Protein.md) are used to build muscle — which only happens if the training gives a reason to. A bulk with poor training mostly adds fat. That means hard [sets](./Set.md), enough [volume](./Volume.md), and steady [progressive overload](./Progressive%20overload.md); lifts should be going up during a bulk.

Most coaches recommend a slow, "lean" bulk — gaining roughly 0.25–0.5% of bodyweight a month for experienced lifters, somewhat more for beginners — to limit fat gain. Beginners can often build muscle without a dedicated bulk through [body recomposition](./Body%20recomposition.md). The eating side — how big a surplus, and what to eat — is covered in the Nutrition Glossary.

_Avoid:_ "dirty bulk" — eating anything and everything usually adds much more fat than muscle.

_Usage:_

"I'm bulking, so I can skip leg day, right?"

"No — a bulk without hard training is just gaining fat."
