description: Low-intensity steady-state cardio: easy, steady exercise at a pace you can hold for a long time, like brisk walking.
---

Stands for **L**ow-**I**ntensity **S**teady-**S**tate: [cardio](./Cardio.md) done at an easy, steady pace for a longer period — brisk walking, easy cycling, a light jog — usually 30–60 minutes. You should be able to hold a conversation.

LISS builds basic endurance, supports heart health, and burns calories without much fatigue, so it interferes very little with lifting and [recovery](./Recovery.md). That's why it's popular during a [cut](./Cutting.md): a daily walk can raise calorie burn without draining energy for training.

It overlaps heavily with [Zone 2](./Zone%202.md) training; LISS is a looser, gym-culture term, while Zone 2 is defined by [heart rate](./Heart%20rate%20zones.md). The opposite approach is [HIIT](./HIIT.md). Most people benefit from mostly easy cardio with a little hard work mixed in.

_Avoid:_ "it's too easy to count" — easy cardio is still real training, and it's the kind most people can do every day.

_Usage:_

"Is walking even real cardio?"

"Yes — brisk walking is LISS. It's great for fat loss because it barely tires you out."
