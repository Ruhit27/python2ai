description: Reps in reserve: how many more reps you could have done when you stopped a set. 2 RIR means two left in the tank.
---

Stands for **R**eps **I**n **R**eserve: the number of extra [reps](./Rep.md) you could have done when you ended a [set](./Set.md). Stopping with 2 reps left in the tank is "2 RIR"; stopping because you couldn't do another is "0 RIR" ([failure](./Training%20to%20failure.md)).

RIR is a simple way to control effort. Many programs aim for 1–3 RIR on most sets: hard enough to drive growth and [strength](./Strength.md), but not so hard that fatigue piles up. It maps directly onto [RPE](./RPE.md): 0 RIR = RPE 10, 1 RIR = RPE 9, 2 RIR = RPE 8.

Like RPE, it takes practice. New lifters often stop with 5 or more reps left while thinking they're near their limit. If a set that "should" be 2 RIR ends up as 8 reps when you could have done 14, the weight was too light for the goal.

_Avoid:_ "going until it burns" — a burning feeling is not a reliable sign of how many reps are left.

_Usage:_

"How hard should my sets be?"

"Most at 1 to 2 RIR — stop when you're confident you could do one or two more with good form."
