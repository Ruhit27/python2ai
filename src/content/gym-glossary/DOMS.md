Stands for **D**elayed **O**nset **M**uscle **S**oreness: the ache and stiffness felt in muscles 24–72 hours after training, especially after new exercises, a big jump in [volume](./Volume.md), or lots of lowering (eccentric) work.

DOMS is normal and harmless. It's strongest when your body meets something unfamiliar — the first week back after a break, or a new [program](./Program.md) — and fades as you get used to the training (the *repeated bout effect*). It doesn't mean you've built more muscle, and a lack of soreness doesn't mean a workout was wasted.

Light movement, [sleep](./Sleep.md), and time are the best cures; most remedies (supplements, massage guns) only mildly ease the feeling. Sharp pain, pain in a joint, or soreness that lasts well over a week isn't DOMS — see [injury and soreness](./Injury%20and%20soreness.md).

_Avoid:_ "lactic acid" as the cause — lactate clears within an hour of exercise; DOMS is caused by small-scale muscle damage and inflammation.

_Usage:_

"I'm not sore anymore after leg day. Is it not working?"

"Your body's adapted to the DOMS, not the training. Check if your lifts are still going up."
