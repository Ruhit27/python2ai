---
description: A movement that works one main muscle through one joint, like a bicep curl or leg extension.
---

An exercise that moves a single joint and mainly targets one muscle. A bicep curl only bends the elbow and mostly trains the biceps; a leg extension only straightens the knee and trains the front of the thigh.

Isolation exercises let you aim [volume](./Volume.md) at a particular [muscle](./Muscle%20group.md) — one that's lagging, or that [compound exercises](./Compound%20exercise.md) don't train fully, like the side of the shoulders or the calves. Because they use lighter weights and less of the body, they're also easier to recover from and safer to take close to [failure](./Training%20to%20failure.md).

They're not "worse" than compounds, just different tools. A program of only isolation exercises takes a long time to train the whole body; a program of only compounds may leave some muscles under-trained. They're also where the [mind-muscle connection](./Mind-muscle%20connection.md) seems to matter most.

_Avoid:_ "toning exercises" — isolation exercises don't tone a muscle differently; they just train it more directly.

_Usage:_

"My arms aren't growing even though I bench and row."

"Add a few sets of curls and triceps extensions. Isolation work will target them directly."
