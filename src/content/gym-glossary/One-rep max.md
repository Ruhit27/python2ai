The heaviest weight you can lift once, with good [form](./Form.md), for a given exercise. Written **1RM**. If you can [squat](./Squat.md) 100 kg (220 lb) for one [rep](./Rep.md) but not 102.5 kg (225 lb), your squat 1RM is 100 kg.

1RM is a way of measuring [strength](./Strength.md) and of setting training weights. Programs often prescribe [intensity](./Intensity.md) as a percentage of it: "5 × 5 at 75%" with a 100 kg max means 75 kg (165 lb). A new best 1RM is a type of [PR](./PR.md).

You don't have to test a true max to know it. Testing can be tiring and risky for beginners, so most people estimate it from a set of several reps — for example, the weight you can lift for 5 reps is roughly 85–87% of your 1RM. Formulas and apps do this calculation, and they're accurate enough for planning.

_Avoid:_ "max" on its own is ambiguous — say whether you mean a one-rep max or a max number of reps.

_Usage:_

"What's your bench max?"

"I've never tested it. I did 80 kg (175 lb) for five, so my estimated one-rep max is around 92 kg (200 lb)."
