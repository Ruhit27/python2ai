---
description: Hanging from a bar and pulling your body up until your chin is over it. A key back and arm exercise.
---

An exercise where you hang from a bar with your palms facing away and pull yourself up until your chin is above the bar. With palms facing you, it's called a *chin-up*, which shifts more work to the biceps. Both mainly train the large back muscles (the *lats*) and the arms.

The pull-up is one of the best upper-body [compound exercises](./Compound%20exercise.md), and it balances pushing lifts like the [bench press](./Bench%20press.md). It's hard for beginners because you lift your whole bodyweight. Common ways to build up to it: the lat pulldown machine, band-assisted pull-ups, or *negatives* (jumping to the top and lowering slowly).

Once bodyweight reps become easy, [progressive overload](./Progressive%20overload.md) can mean adding reps or hanging weight from a belt. Full [range of motion](./Range%20of%20motion.md) — from straight arms to chin over the bar — makes each [rep](./Rep.md) count.

_Avoid:_ "pull-up" and "chin-up" as the same thing — the grip is different, and so is the emphasis.

_Usage:_

"I can't do a single pull-up."

"Start with negatives: jump up, then lower yourself for five seconds. Keep practicing them a few times a week and you'll get there."
