description: Five ranges of heart rate, from very easy to all-out, used to describe and plan how hard cardio should be.
---

A way of dividing exercise effort into (usually five) ranges based on heart rate, as a percentage of your maximum heart rate:

| Zone | % of max HR | Feels like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 50–60% | Very easy, warm-up |
| 2 | 60–70% | Easy, can talk ([Zone 2](./Zone%202.md)) |
| 3 | 70–80% | Moderate, harder to talk |
| 4 | 80–90% | Hard, a few words at a time |
| 5 | 90–100% | All-out, short bursts |

Zones make [cardio](./Cardio.md) plans precise: "30 minutes in Zone 2" or "intervals in Zone 5". Easy zones (1–2) match [LISS](./LISS.md); the top zones are where [HIIT](./HIIT.md) intervals happen.

Maximum heart rate is often estimated as 220 minus your age, but the formula can be off by 10 or more beats per minute for individuals. A heart-rate chest strap is more accurate than a wrist watch. When in doubt, the "talk test" is a decent guide: if you can chat comfortably, you're in the lower zones.

_Avoid:_ "220 minus age" as your true max — it's a rough population average, not a measurement.

_Usage:_

"My watch says I'm in Zone 4 on an easy jog."

"The 220-minus-age estimate might be wrong for you. Try the talk test instead."
