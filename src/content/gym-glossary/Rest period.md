description: The break between sets. Longer rests let you lift more on the next set; shorter rests save time and raise your heart rate.
---

The time you rest between [sets](./Set.md). It lets your muscles partly recover their short-term energy so the next set can be done with good [form](./Form.md) and close to the same weight.

Rest length affects performance. After a heavy set of squats, most people need 2–3 minutes (or more) to repeat it; after a set of arm curls, a minute is often enough. Research suggests that for building muscle, resting longer — around 2 minutes or more for big [compound exercises](./Compound%20exercise.md) — tends to work better than very short rests, because you can do more total work.

Short rests aren't wrong, just different: they make training more time-efficient and more tiring, and they push the heart rate up, which moves the session toward [conditioning](./Conditioning.md). The rest *between workouts*, which lets muscles repair and grow, is a separate thing — see [recovery](./Recovery.md).

_Avoid:_ "shorter rest means a better workout" — feeling more out of breath doesn't mean more muscle is built.

_Usage:_

"Why can I only do 6 reps on my second set when I did 10 on the first?"

"You rested 45 seconds. Try two minutes before your next set."
