---
description: A split that groups training into pushing days, pulling days, and leg days. Often called PPL.
---

A popular [split](./Split.md), often written **PPL**, that divides training into three kinds of days. **Push** days train muscles that push weight away (chest, shoulders, triceps) — [bench press](./Bench%20press.md), [overhead press](./Overhead%20press.md). **Pull** days train muscles that pull weight toward you (back, biceps) — rows, [pull-ups](./Pull-up.md). **Leg** days train the legs — [squats](./Squat.md), lunges, leg curls.

It's usually run either three days a week (each day once) or six days (each day twice). The six-day version trains every muscle twice a week, which fits the research on [frequency](./Training%20frequency.md); the three-day version trains each muscle only once.

PPL groups muscles that work together, so one day's training doesn't tire the next day's muscles much. It suits intermediate lifters with time to train often, but it's more than most beginners need — a [full-body](./Full-body%20workout.md) program is usually simpler.

_Avoid:_ "PPL" as a beginner requirement — it works, but full-body routines are usually better for new lifters.

_Usage:_

"Is push pull legs good?"

"Very — if you can train six days a week. On three days, full-body hits each muscle more often."
