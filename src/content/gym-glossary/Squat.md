A movement where you lower your hips by bending at the knees and hips, then stand back up. In the gym it usually means the *barbell back squat*, with a bar resting on the upper back, but there are many variations: front squat (bar on the front of the shoulders), goblet squat (holding a dumbbell at the chest), and bodyweight squats.

The squat is one of the main [compound exercises](./Compound%20exercise.md). It works the thighs (quadriceps), glutes, and inner thighs, while the back and core work hard to keep the body upright. It's one of the three powerlifting lifts, along with the [bench press](./Bench%20press.md) and [deadlift](./Deadlift.md).

How deep to squat depends on your [mobility](./Mobility.md) and anatomy, but going at least to where the thighs are about parallel with the floor trains more muscle than shallow squats — see [range of motion](./Range%20of%20motion.md). The old worry that deep squats are bad for the knees isn't supported by research for healthy people using good [form](./Form.md).

_Avoid:_ "squats are bad for your knees" — for healthy knees, well-performed squats are safe and can make knees stronger.

_Usage:_

"Do I need to squat to build legs?"

"Not strictly — leg press and lunges work too — but the squat is one of the best tools for it."
