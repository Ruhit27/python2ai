A set of muscles in the same area that usually work together and are trained together. Common gym groupings:

| Group | Includes | Example exercise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chest | Pectorals | [Bench press](./Bench%20press.md) |
| Back | Lats, upper back | [Pull-up](./Pull-up.md), rows |
| Shoulders | Deltoids | [Overhead press](./Overhead%20press.md) |
| Arms | Biceps, triceps | Curls, dips |
| Legs | Quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves | [Squat](./Squat.md), [deadlift](./Deadlift.md) |
| Core | Abdominals, obliques | Planks |

Programs count [volume](./Volume.md) and [frequency](./Training%20frequency.md) per muscle group, and [splits](./Split.md) are organized around them. [Compound exercises](./Compound%20exercise.md) train several groups at once; [isolation exercises](./Isolation%20exercise.md) target one.

A well-rounded program trains every major group, including those you can't see in the mirror — back, hamstrings, glutes. Skipping them leads to imbalances in [strength](./Strength.md) and appearance.

_Avoid:_ "abs" as the whole core — the core also includes the sides and lower back.

_Usage:_

"Do I need to train my back? I can't see it."

"Everyone else can — and it balances all that chest work."
