A phase of eating fewer calories than you burn, to lose body fat — usually after a [bulk](./Bulking.md), or before an event. The goal isn't just to lose weight but to lose *fat* while keeping as much muscle as possible.

From the training side, the key is to keep lifting with the same [intensity](./Intensity.md) — the same heavy weights — as before. Lifting heavy tells the body the muscle is still needed. Many lifters cut [volume](./Volume.md) somewhat, since [recovery](./Recovery.md) is worse on fewer calories, but dropping to light, high-rep "toning" work is a mistake. Eating plenty of [protein](./Protein.md) helps protect muscle too.

Expect [strength](./Strength.md) to hold steady or drop a little during a cut, and [pumps](./Pump.md) to be smaller. [Cardio](./Cardio.md) can help create the calorie gap, but the diet does most of the work — the eating side is covered in the Nutrition Glossary.

_Avoid:_ "spot reduction" — doing sit-ups doesn't burn fat from your belly specifically; fat loss happens across the whole body.

_Usage:_

"I'm cutting, so should I switch to light weights and high reps?"

"No — keep lifting heavy. That's what tells your body to hold on to muscle."
