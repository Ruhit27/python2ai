---
description: The process of rebuilding after training. Muscles actually grow and get stronger during recovery, not during the workout.
---

What happens between workouts: the body repairs the stress from training and adapts — which is when muscles actually grow and get stronger. Training provides the signal; recovery is when the change happens.

The main ingredients are [sleep](./Sleep.md), food (enough calories and [protein](./Protein.md)), and time. Stress, poor sleep, and a big calorie deficit (like during a [cut](./Cutting.md)) all slow recovery. Signs that recovery isn't keeping up include performance dropping, lingering aches, poor sleep, and low motivation.

Programs manage recovery with [rest](./Rest%20period.md) between [sets](./Set.md), rest days between sessions, sensible [volume](./Volume.md), and planned [deloads](./Deload.md). Pushing well past what you can recover from for a long time leads toward [overtraining](./Overtraining.md). Many recovery products — ice baths, massage guns, compression wear — have small effects compared to sleeping and eating well.

_Avoid:_ "recovery" as a product — the biggest recovery tools are free: sleep, food, and rest days.

_Usage:_

"I train six days a week and I'm getting weaker."

"You're not recovering. Fewer training days and more sleep usually turn that around."
