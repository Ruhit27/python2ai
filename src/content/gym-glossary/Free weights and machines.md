Two main types of gym equipment. **Free weights** — barbells, dumbbells, kettlebells — can move in any direction, so your body has to control the path and balance the load. **Machines** — like the leg press or chest press — guide the weight along a fixed path, often using cables or levers.

Free weights train stabilizing muscles and coordination, and let you do the classic [compound lifts](./Compound%20exercise.md) like the [squat](./Squat.md) and [deadlift](./Deadlift.md). Machines are easier to learn, safer to take to [failure](./Training%20to%20failure.md) without a spotter, and good at targeting one muscle (see [isolation exercise](./Isolation%20exercise.md)).

Research shows both build muscle well when used with enough effort and [volume](./Volume.md). The debate over which is "better" matters less than consistency and [progressive overload](./Progressive%20overload.md). Most good programs use both.

_Avoid:_ "machines are for beginners" — machines are used by advanced lifters and bodybuilders too.

_Usage:_

"Is the leg press as good as squats?"

"For building leg muscle, it's close — and easier to push hard safely. Squats build more whole-body strength."
