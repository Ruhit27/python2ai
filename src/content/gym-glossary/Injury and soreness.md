Knowing the difference between normal soreness and an injury is one of the most useful things a beginner can learn.

**Soreness** ([DOMS](./DOMS.md)) is a dull, spread-out ache in the muscle itself, felt a day or two after training. It eases with movement and fades within a few days.

**Injury pain** is usually sharp, localized, and felt during a movement — often in or around a joint — or it persists, gets worse, or comes with swelling, bruising, or loss of strength. A "tweak" in the lower back during a [deadlift](./Deadlift.md) or a sharp shoulder pain on the [bench press](./Bench%20press.md) falls here.

Minor tweaks usually recover by training around them — lighter loads, less painful exercises — rather than resting completely. Sudden jumps in [volume](./Volume.md) or load, poor [form](./Form.md), and training through fatigue are common causes. Sharp pain, numbness, or pain that doesn't improve after a week or two is a reason to see a doctor or physiotherapist.

_Avoid:_ "push through the pain" — pushing through soreness is fine; pushing through sharp or joint pain is how small injuries become big ones.

_Usage:_

"My shoulder twinges on every bench rep."

"That's not soreness. Stop benching for now, try a few pain-free exercises, and see a physio if it doesn't settle."
