description: One complete movement of an exercise, like lowering a weight and lifting it back up once. Short for repetition.
---

Short for *repetition*: one complete movement of an exercise, from start to finish. One squat — down and back up — is one rep. Reps are grouped into [sets](./Set.md): "3 sets of 10" means ten reps, a rest, and then that twice more.

The number of reps you can do with a weight tells you how heavy it is *for you*. A weight you can lift only 3 times is heavy; one you can lift 20 times is light. That's why programs often prescribe a rep range ("8–12 reps") rather than a fixed weight, and why the [one-rep max](./One-rep%20max.md) — the weight you could lift for exactly one rep — is a common measuring stick.

Different rep ranges lean toward different goals. Low reps with heavy weight (1–5) build maximal [strength](./Strength.md) best. Research shows that muscle growth ([hypertrophy](./Muscle%20hypertrophy.md)) happens across a wide range — roughly 6 to 30 reps — as long as sets are taken close to [failure](./Training%20to%20failure.md). Every rep should use good [form](./Form.md); a rep done badly doesn't count for much.

_Avoid:_ "rep" for a set — a rep is one movement; a set is a group of reps in a row.

_Usage:_

"How many reps should I do?"

"For now, pick a weight you can lift about 10 times with good form, and stop when you have one or two left in the tank."
