An exercise that moves more than one joint and works several [muscle groups](./Muscle%20group.md) at the same time. The [squat](./Squat.md) bends the hips, knees, and ankles and works the thighs, glutes, and back; the [bench press](./Bench%20press.md) moves the shoulders and elbows and works the chest, shoulders, and triceps.

Compound exercises let you lift heavy weights and train a lot of muscle in little time, so they form the core of most beginner [programs](./Program.md). The classic "big" ones are the squat, [deadlift](./Deadlift.md), bench press, [overhead press](./Overhead%20press.md), and rows or [pull-ups](./Pull-up.md). They're the best way to build overall [strength](./Strength.md).

The opposite is an [isolation exercise](./Isolation%20exercise.md), which targets one muscle through one joint. Most good programs combine the two: compounds for the bulk of the work, isolation exercises to target muscles the compounds miss or to add [volume](./Volume.md).

_Avoid:_ "functional exercise" as a synonym — compound describes how many joints move, not how useful an exercise is in daily life.

_Usage:_

"I only have 45 minutes. What should I do?"

"Stick to compound exercises: squat, bench, row. You'll hit almost everything."
