---
description: Focusing your attention on the muscle you're training while you lift. Seems to help most on light, isolation exercises.
---

The practice of consciously focusing on the muscle you're trying to work — "feel your chest doing the work" — rather than just moving the weight from A to B.

Research suggests it can increase how active the target muscle is, and some studies found slightly more growth with it on [isolation exercises](./Isolation%20exercise.md) like curls. On heavy [compound exercises](./Compound%20exercise.md) like [squats](./Squat.md), focusing on moving the weight well tends to work better; attention on one muscle can hurt performance there.

For beginners, the mind-muscle connection is mostly useful on exercises where it's hard to feel the right muscle working — such as the back on rows or the rear of the shoulders. Controlling the [tempo](./Tempo.md) and using a weight you can handle make it easier. It's a small tool, not a replacement for [progressive overload](./Progressive%20overload.md).

_Avoid:_ "if you can't feel it, it isn't working" — muscles can grow from exercises where you feel little.

_Usage:_

"I only feel rows in my arms, not my back."

"Go a bit lighter, slow down, and think about pulling your elbows back. That's the mind-muscle connection."
