description: The swollen, tight feeling in a muscle during a workout, caused by extra blood and fluid. Temporary, and not a measure of growth.
---

The full, tight, swollen feeling in a muscle during and after training — especially after moderate-to-high [rep](./Rep.md) [sets](./Set.md) with short [rests](./Rest%20period.md). It happens because working muscles draw in extra blood and fluid, which temporarily swells the muscle cells.

The pump fades within an hour or two. It feels like progress, and some research suggests the cell swelling might play a small part in [hypertrophy](./Muscle%20hypertrophy.md), but it's not a reliable measure of whether a workout built muscle. Heavy [strength](./Strength.md) training often gives little pump yet builds plenty of muscle.

Pumps are more noticeable when you've eaten carbohydrates and are well hydrated. Chasing the pump is harmless and can be motivating, but the drivers of growth are still hard sets, [volume](./Volume.md), and [progressive overload](./Progressive%20overload.md).

_Avoid:_ "no pump, no growth" — the pump is a short-term swelling, not a sign of long-term progress.

_Usage:_

"I didn't get a pump today — was the workout wasted?"

"No. Heavy sets rarely give a big pump, and they still build muscle."
