A workout that trains all the major [muscle groups](./Muscle%20group.md) in one session — typically a squat or leg movement, an upper-body push, an upper-body pull, and sometimes a hinge like the [deadlift](./Deadlift.md).

Full-body training is the most common [split](./Split.md) for beginners. Doing it two or three times a week means each muscle gets trained often ([frequency](./Training%20frequency.md)) and each main lift gets practiced often — which is how beginners improve [form](./Form.md) and [strength](./Strength.md) fastest. It also forgives missed sessions: if you skip one, nothing goes untrained all week.

The downside is that sessions can get long as you add [volume](./Volume.md), and it's hard to fit many exercises per muscle. That's why more advanced lifters often move to upper/lower or [push/pull/legs](./Push%20pull%20legs.md) splits.

_Avoid:_ "full-body means cardio" — a full-body workout usually means strength training for every muscle group.

_Usage:_

"Three full-body days seems like too little."

"It's plenty for a beginner. Every muscle is trained three times a week."
