---
description: A planned lighter week of training to let fatigue fall away, so you come back stronger.
---

A short, planned period — usually a week — of easier training. [Volume](./Volume.md) (fewer sets), [intensity](./Intensity.md) (lighter weights), or both are reduced, often by about half. The goal is to let accumulated fatigue drop so that [strength](./Strength.md) and performance can show up fully afterwards.

Hard training builds up fatigue faster than [recovery](./Recovery.md) clears it, especially in joints and connective tissue. A deload is a controlled way to clear it without stopping training entirely. Many [programs](./Program.md) schedule one every 4–8 weeks, often at the end of a [periodization](./Periodization.md) block; others deload only when signs appear — performance dropping, aches building, motivation fading.

Beginners rarely need planned deloads. For intermediate and advanced lifters, a deload is often the fastest way out of a short-term [plateau](./Plateau.md).

_Avoid:_ "deload" as a week off — you still train, just lighter. Skipping training entirely is a rest week.

_Usage:_

"My lifts are going down and everything aches."

"You're overdue for a deload. Do half your usual sets this week at the same weights."
