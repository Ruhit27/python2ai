description: Personal record: your best-ever performance on a lift, like the most weight or the most reps. Also called PB.

Short for **P**ersonal **R**ecord (also *PB*, personal best): your best-ever result on a lift. It can be the heaviest single [rep](./Rep.md) (a [one-rep max](./One-rep%20max.md) PR), the most reps at a certain weight (a rep PR), or the heaviest weight for a set number of reps (a 5-rep PR).

PRs are the most visible sign of [progressive overload](./Progressive%20overload.md) working. Beginners hit them nearly every session; as training goes on, they come less often, and long gaps without one can signal a [plateau](./Plateau.md).

Rep PRs are the underrated kind. Adding one more rep at the same weight is real progress, and it's safer and less tiring than testing a new max. Many lifters track rep PRs in a log so they can see progress even when the heaviest weight hasn't moved.

_Avoid:_ "PR" as only a max-weight single — a rep PR at a familiar weight counts too.

_Usage:_

"I didn't add weight to my squat this week."

"But you did 8 reps at 80 kg (175 lb) — last time it was 6. That's a rep PR."
