Doing a [set](./Set.md) until you can't complete another [rep](./Rep.md) with good [form](./Form.md). *Technical failure* is when your form starts to break down; *absolute failure* is when the weight won't move at all.

Sets have to be hard to build muscle and [strength](./Strength.md), but they don't have to reach failure. Research suggests that stopping one to three reps short — measured with [RIR](./RIR.md) or [RPE](./RPE.md) — gives nearly the same results with less fatigue. Going to failure on every set, especially on heavy [compound exercises](./Compound%20exercise.md) like [squats](./Squat.md), adds fatigue that can hurt the next sets and the next sessions.

A common approach: stay a rep or two short on big lifts, and take [isolation exercises](./Isolation%20exercise.md) like curls closer to failure, where it's safer. Failing a rep with a heavy bar over you (bench press, squat) should only happen with safety bars or a spotter.

_Avoid:_ "no pain, no gain" — sets need to be hard, not maximally painful.

_Usage:_

"Should every set be to failure?"

"No. Leave one or two reps in reserve on the big lifts. Save failure for the last set of curls."
