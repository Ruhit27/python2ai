How far a joint moves through an exercise — for example, how deep you go in a [squat](./Squat.md), or how far the bar travels in a [bench press](./Bench%20press.md). Often shortened to *ROM*.

In general, using a full range of motion builds more muscle and strength across that range than short, partial [reps](./Rep.md). Recent research also suggests that the part of the movement where the muscle is stretched long — the bottom of a squat, the stretched bottom of a curl — is especially good for growth. Cutting reps short to lift heavier weights usually trades real progress for a bigger number on the bar.

"Full" range depends on the person. Your usable range is limited by your [mobility](./Mobility.md) and anatomy, and good [form](./Form.md) means using as much range as you can control. Partial reps have their place — for example, to work a weak point — but as an extra, not a replacement.

_Avoid:_ "half reps" as a sign of strength — lifting more weight through less range is not the same as getting stronger.

_Usage:_

"Everyone at the gym does quarter squats with huge weight."

"They'd grow more going deeper with less. Full range of motion trains the whole muscle."
