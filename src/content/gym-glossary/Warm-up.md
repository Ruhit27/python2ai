---
description: Light activity and easier sets before training to raise body temperature and practice the movement before heavy lifting.
---

Light activity done before hard training to get the body ready. A good warm-up raises body temperature and heart rate, loosens the joints through their [range of motion](./Range%20of%20motion.md), and practices the movement pattern before the weight gets heavy.

A typical lifting warm-up has two parts. First, a few minutes of general movement — brisk walking, cycling, or light dynamic movements like leg swings and arm circles. Second, **warm-up sets** of the first exercise: if your working [sets](./Set.md) of squats are at 80 kg (175 lb), you might do an empty bar, then 40 kg (90 lb), then 60 kg (130 lb) for a few [reps](./Rep.md) each.

Long static [stretching](./Stretching.md) — holding a stretch for a minute — isn't needed before lifting and can slightly reduce strength right afterwards. Warming up well helps performance and makes it easier to use good [form](./Form.md) from the first working set; claims that it prevents most [injuries](./Injury%20and%20soreness.md) are less certain.

_Avoid:_ "cardio" as a warm-up substitute on its own — five minutes on a bike warms you up generally, but it doesn't practice the lift.

_Usage:_

"Can I just go straight to my working weight on bench?"

"Do two or three lighter sets first. Your first heavy set will feel much better."
