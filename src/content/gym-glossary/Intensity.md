The word has two meanings in the gym, and they're easy to mix up.

**Intensity as load.** In strength training, intensity means how heavy a weight is relative to your [one-rep max](./One-rep%20max.md). Lifting 90% of your max is high intensity; 50% is low. Programs written as "5 × 3 at 85%" are describing intensity this way.

**Intensity as effort.** In everyday gym talk, "intense" means how hard a set feels, or how close to [failure](./Training%20to%20failure.md) it goes. A set of 20 light reps taken to failure is low intensity in the first sense but very high effort. Coaches often call this *intensity of effort* and measure it with [RPE](./RPE.md) or [RIR](./RIR.md).

Both matter. Heavy loads are best for building maximal [strength](./Strength.md); high effort is what makes lighter sets build muscle. Intensity and [volume](./Volume.md) trade off against each other: the heavier the weight, the fewer reps and sets you can do.

_Avoid:_ "intense" as a synonym for "a lot" — high intensity with heavy weights usually means less volume, not more.

_Usage:_

"This program says 80% intensity — does that mean 80% effort?"

"No, 80% of your one-rep max. The effort will still be high."
