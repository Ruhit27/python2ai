---
description: A structured process for identifying assets, threats, vulnerabilities, and possible impacts so risks can be compared and addressed.
---

A structured way of working out what could go wrong and how much it matters, so limited time and money go to the biggest [risks](./Risk.md). It typically asks:

1. What [assets](./Asset.md) do we have, and what are they worth to us?
2. What [threats](./Threat.md) could affect them?
3. What weaknesses could those threats use?
4. How likely is each scenario, and how bad would the impact be?

The result is a ranked list of risks, each with a decision: reduce, transfer, avoid, or accept. A small online shop might find its biggest risk is customer card data and prioritize protecting that over, say, the office printer.

Risk assessments are repeated regularly and whenever something big changes — a new system, a merger, a new type of threat. They feed [vulnerability management](./Vulnerability%20management.md) priorities and are required by most [security frameworks](./Security%20framework.md), and underpin [business continuity](./Business%20continuity.md) planning.

_Avoid:_ "risk assessment" as a one-time exercise — risks change, so assessments must be repeated.

_Usage:_

"We have budget for one project. Which one?"

"Do a quick risk assessment. The unprotected customer database probably beats new office cameras."
