description: A deceptive message or website designed to trick someone into revealing information, opening a harmful file, or taking an unsafe action.

A fake message — usually email, but also texts, chat messages, and calls — designed to trick someone into giving away passwords, opening a harmful attachment, paying a fake invoice, or taking another unsafe action. It often leads to a convincing fake login page.

Phishing messages typically create urgency or fear ("your account will be closed today"), pretend to come from someone trusted (a bank, a delivery company, the CEO), and push you to act quickly. *Spear phishing* is targeted at a specific person, using details about them to seem real.

It's one of the most common ways attacks begin, because it targets people rather than software — a form of [social engineering](./Social%20engineering.md). Defenses include [security awareness](./Security%20awareness.md) training, email filtering, and [MFA](./Multi-factor%20authentication%20%28MFA%29.md) (which limits the damage of a stolen password, strengthening [authentication](./Authentication.md)). Most phishing is sent by opportunistic [threat actors](./Threat%20actor.md) at huge scale.

_Avoid:_ "only careless people fall for phishing" — well-made phishing fools experienced people too; reporting it quickly matters more than blame.

_Usage:_

"I got an email from 'IT' asking me to reset my password through a link."

"Don't click it. Report it — IT will never ask that by email. It's phishing."
