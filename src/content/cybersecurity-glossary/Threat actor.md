---
description: A person or group that may attempt harmful activity against systems or information, whether for profit, influence, espionage, or another motive.
---

The person or group behind a [threat](./Threat.md) — who is actually trying to cause harm. The word "actor" separates the *who* from the *what*.

Threat actors differ mainly by motive and skill:

| Type | Usual motive | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cybercriminals | Money | [Ransomware](./Ransomware.md) gangs, fraud |
| Nation-state groups | Espionage, disruption | Long, patient intrusions; [supply-chain attacks](./Supply-chain%20attack.md) |
| Hacktivists | Political or social causes | Defacing websites |
| Insiders | Revenge, money, or accident | An employee leaking data |

Knowing who is likely to target you shapes defenses. A small business mostly faces opportunistic criminals using [phishing](./Phishing.md) and known weaknesses; a defense contractor must also expect well-funded state groups. [Threat hunters](./Threat%20hunting.md) study how specific actors behave so they can look for their traces.

_Avoid:_ "hacker" as a synonym — many hackers are defenders or researchers; "threat actor" names the harmful ones.

_Usage:_

"Who would bother attacking a 10-person company?"

"Most threat actors aren't choosing you — they send millions of phishing emails and see who bites."
