---
description: The people, processes, and technologies used to protect computers, networks, services, and data from unauthorized access, damage, or disruption.
---

Everything done to keep computers, networks, online services, and data safe from people who shouldn't get in, and from damage or disruption. It covers technology — like a [firewall](./Firewall.md) or [encryption](./Encryption.md) — but just as much people (training staff to spot [phishing](./Phishing.md)) and processes (checking who still needs access).

A closely related term is **information security**: protecting *information* in any form, including paper files and conversations, so it stays private, correct, and available — the [CIA triad](./CIA%20triad.md). Cybersecurity is the part of that concerned with computers and networks. In everyday use the two overlap so much that people often use them interchangeably.

Cybersecurity is mostly about managing [risk](./Risk.md), not eliminating it. A small online shop can't stop every [threat](./Threat.md), but it can protect what matters most (customer data, the payment system) with sensible [security controls](./Security%20control.md) in proportion to what could go wrong.

_Avoid:_ "cybersecurity" as only a technical problem — most incidents involve people or processes, not just software.

_Usage:_

"We bought antivirus, so we're covered on cybersecurity, right?"

"That's one control. We also need backups, MFA, and staff who can spot a phishing email."
