---
description: The plans and capabilities that help an organization continue delivering important services during disruption and restore normal operations afterward.
---

Planning so an organization can keep delivering its most important services during a disruption — a [ransomware](./Ransomware.md) attack, a power cut, a flood, a pandemic — and get back to normal afterwards.

Business continuity starts by asking which services matter most and how long each can be down before serious harm (hours for a hospital's patient records; days for an internal newsletter). Plans then cover alternatives: manual workarounds, backup locations, restoring from [backups](./Backup.md), and who does what. The technical part of restoring IT systems is often called *disaster recovery*.

Plans must be tested — through exercises that walk through a scenario — or they tend to fail when needed. Continuity planning draws on [risk assessment](./Risk%20assessment.md), connects to [incident response](./Incident%20response.md) and [recovery](./Recovery.md), and is often required by a [security framework](./Security%20framework.md).

_Avoid:_ "business continuity" as only IT backups — it also covers people, processes, suppliers, and communication.

_Usage:_

"If our systems go down for a week, how do we keep taking orders?"

"That's what the business continuity plan is for — ours says phone orders and paper forms."
