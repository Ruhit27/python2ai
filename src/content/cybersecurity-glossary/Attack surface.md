---
description: The set of places where an attacker could try to enter, interact with, or affect a system, including exposed services, accounts, devices, and people.
---

All the points where an attacker could try to get in or cause harm: websites and services reachable from the internet, user accounts, open network ports, laptops and phones, third-party connections — and people, who can be tricked by [phishing](./Phishing.md) or [social engineering](./Social%20engineering.md).

The bigger the attack surface, the more there is to defend. Every new app, forgotten test server, or old employee account still active adds to it. A company might discover it has a dozen forgotten websites, each a possible way in.

Reducing the attack surface is one of the most effective defenses: turn off services you don't need ([secure configuration](./Secure%20configuration.md)), remove old accounts, and separate systems with [network segmentation](./Network%20segmentation.md) so one weakness doesn't expose everything. It starts with knowing what [assets](./Asset.md) you have and which [vulnerabilities](./Vulnerability.md) they carry.

_Avoid:_ "attack surface" as only technology — staff email inboxes are part of it too.

_Usage:_

"Why shut down the old customer portal? Nobody uses it."

"Attackers might. It's unpatched attack surface with a login page."
