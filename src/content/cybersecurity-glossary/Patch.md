description: A software update that corrects defects or security weaknesses, or otherwise changes a product; applying patches helps reduce exposure to known issues.
---

An update that fixes problems in software — including security [vulnerabilities](./Vulnerability.md). The update prompts on your phone and laptop are patches. Many vendors release them on a regular schedule, plus emergency ones for urgent flaws.

Patching is one of the most effective security habits, because most attacks use known weaknesses that already have a fix. Once a patch is published, attackers study what it fixed and build [exploits](./Exploit.md) for systems that haven't installed it yet — so delay is dangerous.

In organizations, patching is harder than on a personal phone: updates must be tested so they don't break important systems, and there can be thousands of devices. Doing it systematically, prioritized by risk, is part of [vulnerability management](./Vulnerability%20management.md). No patch exists for a [zero-day](./Zero-day%20vulnerability.md) — until the vendor releases one.

_Avoid:_ "we'll patch when we have time" — known, unpatched vulnerabilities are one of the most common ways attackers get in.

_Usage:_

"The update will restart the server in the middle of the day."

"Schedule it for tonight — but don't skip it. That patch fixes a flaw attackers are already using."
