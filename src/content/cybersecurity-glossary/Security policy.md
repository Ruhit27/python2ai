---
description: An organization's documented rules and expectations for protecting systems and information and assigning security responsibilities.
---

An organization's written rules for protecting its systems and information, and for who is responsible for what. It sets expectations for everyone: how passwords must be managed, who can access which data, how devices must be protected, and how to report a problem.

Policies are usually layered: a short, high-level policy approved by leadership, supported by more detailed standards and procedures (for example, "laptops must use disk [encryption](./Encryption.md)"). They define [data classification](./Data%20classification.md) rules and which [security controls](./Security%20control.md) apply.

A policy only works if people know it and can follow it. Unrealistic rules — like changing passwords every week — get ignored or worked around. Good policies are clear, practical, reviewed regularly, and often built on a [security framework](./Security%20framework.md). They're the backbone of an organization's [cybersecurity](./Cybersecurity.md) program.

_Avoid:_ "policy" as a document to satisfy auditors — if nobody follows it, it protects nothing.

_Usage:_

"Can I use my personal laptop for client work?"

"Check the security policy — it says only company-managed devices can access client data."
