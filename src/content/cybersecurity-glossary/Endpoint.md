---
description: A device that connects to or participates in a network, such as a laptop, phone, server, or workstation.
---

Any device at the "end" of a network connection: laptops, desktops, phones, tablets, servers, and increasingly smart devices like printers and cameras. Endpoints are where people work and where data is used — which makes them a favorite target.

Many attacks start on an endpoint: a user opens a malicious attachment, [malware](./Malware.md) installs, and the attacker uses that device as a foothold. Lost or stolen laptops and phones are another common source of data exposure.

Protecting endpoints involves [secure configuration](./Secure%20configuration.md), keeping software patched, disk [encryption](./Encryption.md) in case of theft, and monitoring tools such as [EDR](./Endpoint%20detection%20and%20response%20%28EDR%29.md). Every endpoint is an [asset](./Asset.md) to track — an unmanaged personal laptop connecting to company systems is a common blind spot.

_Avoid:_ "endpoint" as only computers — phones, servers, and smart devices count too.

_Usage:_

"It's just the office printer — why does it need updates?"

"It's an endpoint on our network. Attackers have used printers as a way in."
