A type of [malware](./Malware.md) attack that locks up data or systems — usually by encrypting files — and demands a payment, often in cryptocurrency, to unlock them. Many groups now also steal data first and threaten to publish it, so paying for decryption isn't the only pressure.

Ransomware can halt an entire organization: hospitals diverting patients, factories stopping, schools closing. It usually gets in through [phishing](./Phishing.md), stolen passwords for remote access, or unpatched internet-facing systems, then spreads across the network before triggering.

The most important defense is good [backups](./Backup.md) that the attackers can't reach or delete — kept offline or otherwise isolated — and regularly tested. Together with [segmentation](./Network%20segmentation.md) and quick [incident response](./Incident%20response.md), they can turn a catastrophe into a bad week. Any ransomware infection is a major [security incident](./Security%20incident.md). Authorities generally discourage paying, since payment doesn't guarantee recovery and funds further attacks.

_Avoid:_ "we have backups, so ransomware can't hurt us" — only if the backups are isolated, tested, and the stolen data isn't the bigger problem.

_Usage:_

"Every file on the shared drive now ends in .locked."

"That's ransomware. Unplug from the network and call the incident response line now."
