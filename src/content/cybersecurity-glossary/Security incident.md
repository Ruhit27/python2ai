---

An event that has harmed, or seriously threatens, the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of systems or data — the three parts of the [CIA triad](./CIA%20triad.md). Examples: [ransomware](./Ransomware.md) encrypting files, an attacker logging in with a stolen password, customer data emailed to the wrong person, or a website knocked offline.

Incidents are the confirmed version of what a [security alert](./Security%20alert.md) suggests. Not every alert is an incident, and not every incident starts with an alert — many are reported by staff, customers, or even attackers themselves.

When one is confirmed, an organization follows its [incident response](./Incident%20response.md) plan to limit the damage and restore normal operations ([recovery](./Recovery.md)). Some incidents, especially those involving personal data, also carry legal obligations to notify authorities or affected people within set time limits. A [threat](./Threat.md) only becomes an incident when it actually causes, or seriously threatens, harm.

_Avoid:_ "incident" as only a hack — an accidental data leak or an outage caused by a mistake can be a security incident too.

_Usage:_

"I accidentally sent the salary spreadsheet to a supplier. Is that a big deal?"

"It's a security incident — report it now. The faster we act, the more we can limit it."
