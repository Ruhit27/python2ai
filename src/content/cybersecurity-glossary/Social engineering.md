---
description: Manipulating people into disclosing information or performing actions that weaken security, often by using urgency, authority, or trust.
---

Manipulating people — rather than hacking computers — into giving away information or doing something that weakens security. It exploits natural human tendencies: wanting to help, trusting authority, reacting to urgency, and avoiding awkwardness.

Examples: a caller pretending to be IT support asks for your password; someone in a delivery uniform follows staff through a secure door ("tailgating"); a fake "CEO" email urgently asks finance to wire money. [Phishing](./Phishing.md) is the most common form.

Because it targets people, technology alone can't stop it. [Security awareness](./Security%20awareness.md) training, clear procedures (always verify payment changes by phone using a known number), and a culture where it's fine to say "let me check" are the main defenses. [Threat actors](./Threat%20actor.md) often research their targets on social media first, so their story sounds convincing.

_Avoid:_ "social engineering" as social media hacking — it means manipulating people, by any channel.

_Usage:_

"Someone called saying they're from our bank and asked me to confirm the account login."

"Hang up and call the bank's official number. That's classic social engineering."
