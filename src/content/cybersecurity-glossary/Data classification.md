---
description: Labeling information by sensitivity or importance so appropriate handling and protection requirements can be applied.
---

Sorting information into categories by how sensitive or important it is, so each gets the right protection. A common scheme:

| Label | Example | Handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public | Marketing brochures | No restrictions |
| Internal | Team meeting notes | Staff only |
| Confidential | Customer lists, contracts | Limited access, [encrypted](./Encryption.md) |
| Restricted | Health records, passwords | Strict access, extra controls |

Without classification, organizations either over-protect everything (expensive and frustrating) or under-protect what matters. Classification tells people how to handle each document — whether they can email it externally, store it on a personal device, or print it.

It's an early step in protecting [assets](./Asset.md), usually defined in a [security policy](./Security%20policy.md). It applies to information in every format — paper included — not just digital files, which is the wider idea of information security (see [cybersecurity](./Cybersecurity.md)).

_Avoid:_ "everything is confidential" — if every file is top secret, people stop taking labels seriously.

_Usage:_

"Can I upload this spreadsheet to a free online converter?"

"Check the label. It's marked Confidential — so no."
