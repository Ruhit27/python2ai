description: Helping people recognize security responsibilities and common risks, and know how to report or avoid unsafe activity.
---

Helping everyone in an organization understand common security risks, their own responsibilities, and what to do when something seems wrong. Since many attacks target people — through [phishing](./Phishing.md) and [social engineering](./Social%20engineering.md) — informed staff are an important line of defense.

Good awareness programs are short, frequent, and practical: how to spot a suspicious email, why [MFA](./Multi-factor%20authentication%20%28MFA%29.md) matters, how to handle sensitive data under the [security policy](./Security%20policy.md), and — most importantly — how and where to report concerns quickly without fear of blame. Simulated phishing tests are common, but work best as learning, not punishment.

The goal isn't perfection — everyone will eventually click something. The goal is fast reporting, so a single mistake becomes a small, quickly handled event rather than a major [security incident](./Security%20incident.md).

_Avoid:_ "awareness training" as a yearly slideshow — short, frequent, practical sessions change behavior far more.

_Usage:_

"I clicked a phishing link. Will I get in trouble?"

"No — you reported it in five minutes. That's exactly what security awareness training is for."
